The Byeongja Horan — 45 Days of War and the Humiliation of Samjeondo

December 1636. Hong Taiji's 120,000-strong Qing army invaded Joseon. King Injo surrendered after 45 days and bowed three times at Samjeondo.

Era Joseon Topic War Related China (Qing)

Overview

The Byeongja Horan (丙子胡亂, 1636–1637) was a Qing invasion of Joseon. Hong Taiji, Emperor Taizong of Qing, led an army of 120,000 troops into Korea on December 1, 1636. King Injo (仁祖), cut off from his planned refuge on Ganghwa Island, retreated to Namhansanseong fortress and held out for 45 days before submitting to Emperor Taizong at Samjeondo (三田渡, in present-day Songpa, Seoul) on January 30, 1637, performing the sambaegugoduryе — three full prostrations with nine head-knockings to the ground.

Causes of the War

The Later Jin (後金, later Qing) had established a "fraternal" relationship with Joseon through the first Manchu invasion (Jeongmyo Horan) in 1627. However, when Hong Taiji proclaimed himself emperor in 1636, hard-line anti-appeasement officials at the Joseon court vigorously refused to render the subject's protocol to a "barbarian emperor." In response, Qing invaded to compel Joseon to acknowledge its subordinate status.

The Surrender at Samjeondo and Its Aftermath

The surrender terms were harsh. Joseon became a vassal state of Qing, and Crown Prince Sohyeon and Prince Bongnim were taken as hostages to Shenyang. Qing demanded the deployment of Joseon troops for its campaigns against the Ming dynasty. An estimated 500,000 Koreans were taken captive. A stele commemorating Hong Taiji's virtue was erected at Samjeondo (the "Daecheong Hwangje Gongdeokbi"), buried in 1956, then excavated and restored in 1963 — it stands today near Lotte World Tower.

Key Facts

Duration: December 1, 1636 – January 30, 1637 (45 days) · Qing forces: approximately 120,000 · Surrender: Samjeondo · Hostages: Crown Prince Sohyeon and Prince Bongnim · Korean captives: estimated ~500,000
