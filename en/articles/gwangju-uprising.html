The May 18th Gwangju Democratization Movement — Ten Days of Resistance, Forty Years of Truth

May 18–27, 1980. The citizens of Gwangju fought for ten days against martial law troops. Official death toll: 165. The full truth is still being uncovered.

Era Modern Topic Democracy & Resistance Region Gwangju & South Jeolla

Overview

The May 18th Democratization Movement was a pro-democracy uprising that took place in Gwangju from May 18 to 27, 1980. When Chun Doo-hwan's new military junta expanded martial law nationwide and suppressed the democracy movement, Gwangju citizens and students rose in resistance. The excessive use of force by martial law troops and mass shootings killed many people. The uprising's records were inscribed as a UNESCO Memory of the World in 2011.

Ten Days of Events

May 17: Nationwide expansion of martial law → May 18: Airborne troops begin suppression in front of Chonnam National University → Citizen resistance spreads → May 21: Mass shooting in front of the Provincial Office building; citizen militia forms → May 22–26: Citizen militia controls Gwangju → May 27 (early morning): Martial law troops retake the Provincial Office; uprising ends. The official death toll is 165, but the actual number of deaths and disappearances is believed to be higher.

Historical Significance

The May 18th uprising became the spiritual foundation of the June 1987 Democracy Movement. Questions about the full truth remain ongoing — in 2017 controversy erupted when Chun Doo-hwan's memoir denied that military helicopters had fired on protesters. May 18 has been designated as a national memorial day.

Key Facts

Duration: May 18–27, 1980 (10 days) · Official deaths: 165 · Wounded: approximately 3,000 · UNESCO Memory of the World: 2011 · National Memorial Day: May 18
