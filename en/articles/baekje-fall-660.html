The Fall of Baekje and the Battle of Hwangsanbeol — Gyebaek's 5,000 Last Stand

660 CE. A 130,000-strong Silla–Tang alliance invaded. General Gyebaek held Hwangsanbeol with 5,000 men. They won four times — and fell on the fifth. Three days later, Baekje was gone.

Era Three Kingdoms Topic War · Figures

Overview

Baekje (百濟, 18 BCE–660 CE) was a kingdom that ruled the southwestern Korean Peninsula during the Three Kingdoms period. It was destroyed in 660 CE by a joint Silla–Tang alliance. The last king was Uija (義慈王). In the Battle of Hwangsanbeol in July 660, General Gyebaek (階伯) led 5,000 elite troops against Kim Yu-sin's 50,000 Silla soldiers and achieved four consecutive victories, but ultimately fell in defeat. With the capture of Sabi (present-day Buyeo), 678 years of Baekje history came to an end.

The Battle of Hwangsanbeol

On the 9th day of the 7th lunar month, 660, Gyebaek is said to have killed his own wife and children before going into battle — reasoning that it was better they die than become prisoners and slaves of the enemy. His force repelled the Silla army four times. The famous death of Hwarang warrior Gwanchang (官昌), who charged twice and was beheaded, is said to have inflamed the fighting spirit of the Silla troops, and Gyebaek's last stand was annihilated.

Aftermath

In August 660, King Uija surrendered to Tang China. Taken to the Tang capital, he died that year in Luoyang. A Baekje restoration movement (660–663) arose but was crushed at the Battle of Baekgang, where Japanese reinforcements also suffered defeat.

Key Facts

Baekje's existence: 18 BCE–660 CE (678 years) · Battle of Hwangsanbeol: July 660 · Gyebaek's force: 5,000 · Fall of Sabi: August 660 · King Uija died after surrender: 660
