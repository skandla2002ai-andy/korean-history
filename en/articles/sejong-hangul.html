King Sejong and Hunminjeongeum — Why a King Designed an Alphabet

28 letters, created by Sejong in 1443. Why did a king personally design a writing system — and why did his own scholars oppose it?

Era Joseon Topic People Topic Culture Topic Science & Technology

Overview

Hunminjeongeum (訓民正音) is the writing system created personally by King Sejong (世宗大王) in 1443 (the 25th year of his reign) and promulgated in 1446. The name means "the correct sounds for instructing the people." The script originally comprised 28 letters; today 24 are in use (14 consonants, 10 vowels). Sejong stated his reasons for the creation in the preface: "The speech of our country differs from that of China and does not suit Chinese characters. Therefore many of our ignorant people, having something they wish to put into words, are in the end unable to express themselves."

The Political Context of Its Creation

The creation of Hunminjeongeum was not simply a cultural project. At the time, the scholar-official class (sadaebu) held a monopoly on Chinese characters and thus on written power. Ordinary people were entirely helpless in lawsuits, contracts, and official petitions without knowledge of Chinese. Sejong recognized this as a problem of governance: if those who could read monopolized the law, royal authority itself could never be complete.

Choe Manri, Deputy Director of the Hall of Worthies (Jiphyeonjeon), and others submitted a petition of opposition in 1444. Their argument: "As a nation that serves China, using different characters is the way of barbarians." It was also resistance to losing the scholarly class's monopoly on literacy. Sejong personally interrogated them and released them the same day — yet did not stop the project.

Design Principles

Hunminjeongeum is grounded in systematic linguistic theory. Consonants were modeled on the shape of the vocal organs (ㄱ represents the root of the tongue blocking the throat; ㄴ represents the tongue touching the upper gum, etc.). Vowels embody the philosophical trinity of sky (·), earth (ㅡ), and humanity (ㅣ). In 1997, UNESCO registered the Hunminjeongeum Haerye (Explanations and Examples) as a Memory of the World.

Key Facts
