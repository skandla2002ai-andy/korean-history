The March First Movement — Two Million Voices for Independence

March 1, 1919. Thirty-three national representatives read the Declaration of Independence. The cry spread across the entire country.

Era Japanese Colonial Period Topic Independence Movement Related Japan

Overview

The March First Movement (三一運動, 1919) was the largest independence movement of the Japanese colonial era. On March 1, 1919, Son Byong-hui (孫秉熙) and 32 other national representatives read the Declaration of Independence at Taehwagwan restaurant in Seoul, while students and citizens simultaneously gathered at Tapgol Park to cry out for independence. The movement spread across the country over roughly two months, with a cumulative participation of over two million people. Japanese suppression by force resulted in many casualties.

Background — Wilson's Self-Determination

After the end of World War I, U.S. President Woodrow Wilson proclaimed the principle of national self-determination — the right of each people to decide their own destiny. This opened a vision of independence for Korean intellectuals. The sudden death of Emperor Gojong in January 1919 (with widespread rumors of poisoning) also inflamed public sentiment.

The Movement and Japanese Suppression

The movement was organized through a coalition of Christian, Cheondogyo, and Buddhist communities. After March 1, protests erupted in 211 of the country's 218 counties across Gyeonggi, Chungcheong, Gyeongsang, and Jeolla provinces. Japanese authorities deployed military and police forces, firing on crowds and burning villages. In the Jeam-ri Massacre (Hwaseong, Gyeonggi), villagers were locked inside a church and burned alive. Even the official Government-General tally recorded 553 dead, 1,409 wounded, and 12,522 arrested.

Key Facts

Dates: March 1 – May 1919 · National representatives: 33 · Participants: over 2 million (cumulative) · Deaths: 553+ (Government-General figure; actual toll higher) · Outcome: Establishment of the Provisional Government (April 1919); Japan's shift to a "cultural policy" in Korea
