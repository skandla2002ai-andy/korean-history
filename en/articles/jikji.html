Jikji — The World's Oldest Metal-Type Printed Book, 78 Years Before Gutenberg

Printed in Cheongju, Goryeo in 1377 using metal movable type, this book is the oldest surviving metal-type printed work in the world. It is currently held in Paris.

Region Cheongju · Heungdeok Temple Era Goryeo Related France Topic Technology Topic Culture

Overview

The world's oldest surviving metal-type printed book. Printed in 1377 — 78 years before Gutenberg's 42-line Bible (1455).

Baegun hwasang chorok buljo jikji simche yojeol (白雲和尙抄錄佛祖直指心體要節), abbreviated as "Jikji," was printed using metal movable type in July 1377 at Heungdeok Temple in Cheongju, Chungcheong Province, Goryeo. It was inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2001. Today, only the second volume survives and is held at the Bibliothèque nationale de France in Paris.

The Invention of Metal Movable Type

Metal movable type itself was invented in Goryeo earlier than Jikji. Records in the collected works of Yi Gyu-bo mention the Sangjeong gogeum yemun (詳定古今禮文) being printed with metal type in 1234 (the 21st year of King Gojong's reign). However, since no physical copy of that work survives, Jikji — which does survive — holds the title of the world's oldest metal-type printed book.

The background to Goryeo's invention of metal movable type is thought to involve a combination of factors: the need to mass-distribute Buddhist scriptures, advances in bronze casting technology, and the large-scale destruction of woodblocks during the Mongol invasions of the 13th century.

Contents of Jikji

The Jikji simche yojeol is a compilation of core teachings from Seon (Chan) Buddhism. The monk Baegun Gyeonghan (白雲和尙 景閑, 1299–1374) compiled and edited dharma sayings, verses, and letters from successive Seon masters. The title phrase "Jikji simche" (直指心體) means "pointing directly at the essence of the mind."

Three years after Baegun's death, his disciples printed the work at Heungdeok Temple in 1377 in order to spread their teacher's teachings more widely.

The Journey to Paris

Victor Collin de Plancy, a French diplomat stationed in Joseon in the late 19th century, acquired the book during his posting — likely around 1887. It appeared at auction in 1911 as part of de Plancy's collection, where Henri Vever purchased it. After Vever's death in 1950, it was donated to the Bibliothèque nationale de France, where it remains today.

The whereabouts of the first volume remain unknown.

UNESCO Memory of the World

In 2001, UNESCO inscribed Jikji on the Memory of the World Register. To commemorate this, UNESCO established the "Jikji Prize," awarded to organizations and institutions that have contributed to improving access to information.

Key Facts

  • Period: 3rd year of King U of Goryeo (1377)
  • Location: Heungdeok Temple, Cheongju
  • Current location: Bibliothèque nationale de France (Paris)
  • World's oldest surviving metal-type printed book — 78 years before Gutenberg's Bible (1455)
  • UNESCO Memory of the World: inscribed 2001
