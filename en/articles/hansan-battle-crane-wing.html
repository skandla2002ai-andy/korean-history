Battle of Hansan Island — Yi Sun-sin's Crane Wing Formation

August 1592, Hansan Island. Yi Sun-sin used the crane wing formation to destroy over 70 Japanese warships in a decisive naval victory.

Era Joseon Topic War Topic People Related Japan

Overview

The Battle of Hansan Island (閑山島大捷) was fought on August 14, 1592, off the coast of Hansan Island in Gyeongsang Province, when the Joseon naval commander Yi Sun-sin decisively defeated the Japanese navy. One of the three great victories of the Imjin War, it saw approximately 70 Japanese warships sunk or captured through the innovative hakikjin (鶴翼陣, "crane wing formation") encirclement tactic. By securing naval supremacy, Joseon blocked Japan's advance into the Yellow Sea and turned the tide of the war.

Japan had captured Hanyang (Seoul) within a month of the war's opening and was pressing relentlessly northward. On land, however, Yi Sun-sin's Joseon navy had already blunted Japanese momentum at the Battles of Okpo, Sacheon, and Dangpo, winning consecutive victories at sea.

The Crane Wing Tactic and Course of Battle

In August 1592, a Japanese fleet of 73 ships under Wakizaka Yasuharu gathered at Gyeonaeryang (Gyeonnaeryang) to advance against the Jeolla province seaways. Yi Sun-sin judged that the narrow straits of Gyeonaeryang were unsuitable for maneuvering his larger panokson warships, so he sent a small force as bait to lure the Japanese fleet into the open waters off Hansan Island. Once the Japanese fleet moved into open sea, the Joseon navy immediately fanned out to both sides, forming the semicircular hakikjin.

The crane wing formation surrounds the enemy in a semicircle and concentrates cannon fire. Joseon's panokson warships had numerous gun ports along their sides, making them ideal for broadside fire; large cannons including the Cheonjachongtong and Jijachongtong smashed Japanese vessels at long range. Japanese troops, skilled in close-quarters combat and boarding, were unable to close with the Joseon ships before being overwhelmed by cannon fire. Of Wakizaka's 73 ships, 59 were sunk or captured; Wakizaka himself barely escaped.

Historical Significance

The Battle of Hansan Island cut off Japan's route to the Yellow Sea, protecting the tax grain and supply routes of Jeolla and Chungcheong provinces. Without resupply, the Japanese army could not sustain its advance northward, and the war shifted to a prolonged conflict. Yi Sun-sin is said to have later remarked that had he failed to stop the enemy at Hansan Island, the outcome of the war would have been different. The crane wing formation became a model for naval combat, and Yi's strategic genius is highly regarded in East Asian military history. Today, Jeseungdang Hall has been restored in Tongyeong, South Gyeongsang Province, and an annual ceremony commemorates the Battle of Hansan Island.
