Euiyeoldan — Independence Fighters with Bombs and Pistols

Founded in 1919. The Euiyeoldan, led by Kim Won-bong, carried out armed resistance targeting Japanese officials, collaborators, and colonial institutions.

Era Japanese Colonial Topic Independence Movement Related Japan

Overview

The Euiyeoldan (義烈團, "Righteous Fierce Corps") was an anti-Japanese secret organization founded in November 1919 in Jilin (吉林), Manchuria, under the leadership of Kim Won-bong (金元鳳, 1898–1958). True to its name — meaning "to carry out righteous deeds with fierce resolve" — the organization systematically executed acts of assassination and sabotage. Shin Chae-ho's "Declaration of the Korean Revolution" (1923) served as the Euiyeoldan's ideological manifesto.

Major Operations

Notable actions include Park Jae-hyeok's bomb attack on the Busan Police Station (1920), Choe Su-bong's bombing of the Miryang Police Station (1920), Kim Ik-sang's bomb attack on the Government-General of Korea building (1921), Kim Sang-ok's bombing of the Jongno Police Station (1923), and Na Seok-ju's bomb attacks on the Oriental Development Company and the Industrial Bank of Chosun (1926). The actions of Lee Bong-chang and Yun Bong-gil, often associated with this era, were carried out under Kim Ku's Korean Patriotic Corps, not the Euiyeoldan.

Transformation and Dissolution

From the mid-1920s, Kim Won-bong recognized the limitations of individual acts of violence and shifted toward organized military action. Enrolling at the Whampoa Military Academy in 1926 and establishing the Korean Military and Political Cadre School in 1932 were part of this transition. The Euiyeoldan effectively dissolved in 1935 when it merged into the Korean National Revolutionary Party.

Key Facts

Founded: November 1919, Jilin, Manchuria · Leader: Kim Won-bong · Manifesto: Shin Chae-ho's "Declaration of the Korean Revolution" (1923) · Dissolved: 1935 (merged into Korean National Revolutionary Party) · Key operatives: Park Jae-hyeok, Kim Ik-sang, Kim Sang-ok, Na Seok-ju
