Empress Myeongseong — The Queen Assassinated by Japanese Ronin

In the early hours of October 8, 1895, Japanese ronin stormed Gyeongbok Palace and murdered the queen. It was a clear violation of international law and a diplomatic shock felt around the world.

Era Late Joseon Topic Figures · Diplomacy Related Japan

Overview

Empress Myeongseong (明成皇后, 1851–1895) was the queen consort of King Gojong. On October 8, 1895, under the direction of Japanese Minister to Korea Miura Goro, Japanese ronin (hired assassins) broke into Gyeongbok Palace, murdered the queen, and burned her body — an incident known as the Eulmi Incident (乙未事變). Following the proclamation of the Korean Empire in 1897, she was posthumously honored with the title Empress Myeongseong.

Political Background

After the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–1895), Japan's grip on Korea tightened. King Gojong and Queen Min chose to draw in Russia to check Japanese influence, adopting a pro-Russian policy. Threatened by this shift, Japanese Minister Miura resorted to extreme measures. Ronin scaled the walls of Gyeongbok Palace, found the queen, and killed her. The incident was witnessed and recorded by foreign diplomats on the scene.

International Fallout

The Eulmi Incident was a manifest crime under international law. Testimonies from American, Russian, and British diplomats survive. The Japanese government recalled Miura and put him on trial, but he was acquitted for lack of evidence. In the immediate aftermath of the incident, King Gojong took refuge in the Russian legation (the "Agwan Pacheon," 1896).

Key Facts

Dates: 1851–1895 (age 44) · Assassination: October 8, 1895 (Eulmi Incident) · Orchestrated by: Japanese Minister Miura Goro and ronin · King Gojong's refuge at Russian legation: 1896 · Posthumous title: Empress Myeongseong (1897)
