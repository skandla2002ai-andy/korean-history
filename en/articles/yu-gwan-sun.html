Yu Gwan-sun — A Young Girl's Cry for Independence

1902–1920. Ewha Hakdang student Yu Gwan-sun led independence demonstrations at Aunae Marketplace in her hometown of Cheonan. She died in prison at the age of 18.

Era Japanese Colonial Period Topic Independence Movement Topic Figures Related Japan

Overview

Yu Gwan-sun (柳寬順, 1902–1920) was an independence activist who participated in the March First Movement. Born in Cheonan, South Chungcheong Province, she joined the March 1, 1919 demonstrations in Seoul while enrolled at Ewha Hakdang. She then returned to her hometown and led the Aunae Marketplace (Byeongcheon Market) independence rally on April 1. Her parents were killed by Japanese military police during the protest, and she herself was arrested. She continued to call out independence slogans while imprisoned and subjected to torture at Seodaemun Prison, and died on September 28, 1920, at the age of 18.

The Aunae Marketplace Protest

After her school was forcibly closed, Yu Gwan-sun returned home and organized demonstrations with local church leaders. On April 1, some 3,000 to 4,000 people gathered at Aunae Marketplace, waving Taegukgi flags and shouting "Manse!" Japanese military police opened fire, killing more than twenty and wounding over thirty. Yu Gwan-sun's parents, Yu Jung-gwon and Yi So-je, also died that day. Yu Gwan-sun was arrested on the spot.

Imprisonment, Resistance, and Death

She was sentenced to three years in prison at the Seoul District Court appellate hearing and incarcerated at Seodaemun Prison. Even behind bars, she shouted independence slogans on the first anniversary of the March First Movement — March 1, 1920 — and suffered severe torture as a result. She died on September 28, 1920, from malnutrition and the after-effects of torture. Her remains were recovered by Ewha Hakdang and buried in Itaewon, but were later disturbed, and the exact location of her grave today is unknown.

Key Facts

Dates: 1902–1920 (age 18) · Affiliation: Ewha Hakdang · Aunae Marketplace protest: April 1, 1919 · Imprisonment: Seodaemun Prison · Posthumous honor: Order of Independence Merit (1962; elevated to Order of Merit for National Foundation in 2019)
