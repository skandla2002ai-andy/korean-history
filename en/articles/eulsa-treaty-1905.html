Eulsa Treaty 1905 — The Day Korea Lost Its Diplomacy

The Eulsa Treaty of 1905, coerced by Japan, stripped the Korean Empire of its diplomatic sovereignty and installed the Residency-General.

Era Korean Empire Topic Diplomacy Topic History Related Japan

Overview

The Eulsa Treaty (乙巳勒約), also known as the Japan–Korea Protectorate Treaty, was signed under duress on November 17, 1905. It stripped the Korean Empire of its diplomatic sovereignty and established the Residency-General (統監府) in Seoul. The treaty's formal Korean title was the "Korea–Japan Agreement," but because Emperor Gojong never affixed the imperial seal and the signing was coerced, Korean historiography calls it a "forced treaty" (勒約). Through this treaty the Korean Empire was effectively reduced to a Japanese protectorate.

Background

Having won the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905), Japan secured international recognition of its paramount interest in the Korean peninsula through the Treaty of Portsmouth. Japan's plenipotentiary Itō Hirobumi arrived in Seoul in November 1905 and pressured Emperor Gojong to sign. When Gojong refused, Itō coerced the cabinet ministers individually. Five ministers — including Yi Wan-yong and Yi Ji-yong — signed the treaty; they are remembered in Korean history as the "Eulsa Five Traitors" (乙巳五賊).

Treaty Content and Resistance

The five-article treaty placed Korean foreign affairs under Japan's Foreign Ministry and installed Itō Hirobumi as the first Resident-General in Seoul. Immediately after the signing, journalist Jang Ji-yeon published the editorial "This Day I Weep Aloud" in the Hwangseong Sinmun, and Min Yeong-hwan protested by taking his own life. Righteous armies (Eulsa Uibyeong) rose across the country, and Emperor Gojong dispatched secret envoys to plead for international recognition of the treaty's invalidity.

Historical Significance

The scholarly consensus holds that the Eulsa Treaty is void ab initio under international law, as it lacked the emperor's ratification seal and was concluded under coercion. The 1965 Treaty on Basic Relations between Japan and the Republic of Korea agreed that the earlier treaties were "already null and void," but disagreement over whether they were void from the outset or only retroactively remains a contested point in Korean–Japanese historical understanding. The Eulsa Treaty is remembered as the pivotal event that gave rise to the March First Movement, the establishment of the Provisional Government, and the entire arc of Korea's independence struggle.
