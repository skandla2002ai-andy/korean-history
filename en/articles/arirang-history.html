The History of Arirang — Where Did the Nation's Song Come From?

The origins of Arirang and its regional variants. The history of a folk song that became a symbol of resistance during the Japanese colonial period.

Era Late Joseon Topic Culture Topic History

The Origins and Meaning of Arirang

Arirang is Korea's most representative folk song, recognized by its refrain: "Arirang, arirang, arariyo, crossing over Arirang Pass." The exact etymology of the word "arirang" has not been definitively established. Various interpretations exist: some suggest it means "beautiful beloved," others trace it to Alyeong (wife of Silla's founder, Park Hyeokgeose), and still others derive it from the Chinese characters 我耳聾 (my ears are deaf). Arirang first appeared in written records in the late 19th century, but as an oral folk song it is believed to have a much longer history. "Arirang Pass" functions not only as a physical mountain pass but as an emotional space symbolizing life's hardships and the pain of separation.

Types and Regional Variations of Arirang

Arirang has been passed down in various versions reflecting the character of different regions. The most widely known version, "Bonjjo (Standard) Arirang," originates from the Seoul-Gyeonggi region and spread nationwide through director Na Wun-gyu's 1926 silent film Arirang. "Jeongseon Arirang" from Gangwon Province features a slow, lyrical melody with a distinctively deep sorrow and was separately inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2015. "Miryang Arirang" from South Gyeongsang Province is characterized by an upbeat rhythm, while "Jindo Arirang" from Jeolla Province showcases the distinctive features of southern Korean music with its lively, undulating melody. To date, more than 60 types and some 3,600 verses of Arirang have been collected.

Arirang During the Japanese Colonial Period — A Song of Resistance and Nationhood

Na Wun-gyu's 1926 film Arirang captured the national anguish of Koreans living under Japanese rule, moving audiences across the country to tears upon its release. The film became the decisive turning point that branded Arirang as a symbol of national resistance. Japanese colonial authorities subsequently restricted public performances of Arirang on the grounds that it stirred nationalist sentiment. Korean diaspora communities maintained emotional ties to their homeland by singing Arirang abroad, and it was sung at independence movement gatherings as well.

UNESCO Inscription and the Present Day

Arirang was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2012. As a cultural heritage shared by both Koreas, it has served as the official theme song when unified Korean teams enter international sporting events. Today, Arirang continues to live on through K-pop reinterpretations, classical arrangements, jazz adaptations, and countless other forms.
