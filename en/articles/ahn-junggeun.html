Ahn Jung-geun's Vision of East Asian Peace — Assassin or Political Thinker?

At Harbin Station in 1909, Ahn Jung-geun shot Itō Hirobumi. Until his execution, he wrote his Treatise on Peace in East Asia — left unfinished, yet revealing the mind of a political philosopher.

Era Korean Empire Topic Independence Movement Topic People Related Japan

The Harbin Incident (October 26, 1909)

Ahn Jung-geun (安重根, 1879–1910) carried out the action in his self-declared capacity as a lieutenant general of the Korean Righteous Army. When Itō Hirobumi arrived at Harbin Station, Ahn stepped forward from among the welcoming crowd and fired three shots. Itō died at the scene. As he was arrested, Ahn shouted in Russian: "Koryo ura!" — "Long live Korea!"

Among the fifteen charges Ahn cited as grounds for the shooting were the assassination of Empress Myeongseong, the forced conclusion of the Eulsa Treaty, the abdication of the Korean emperor, and the massacre of innocent Koreans.

The Core Vision of the Treatise on Peace in East Asia

After being sentenced to death, Ahn wrote his Treatise on Peace in East Asia (東洋平和論) in Lushun Prison. Its central proposals were:

  • A peace alliance among Korea, China, and Japan, pledging mutual non-aggression
  • Converting Lushun (Port Arthur) into a jointly administered peace city under the three nations
  • Establishment of a joint bank and issuance of a common currency by the three nations
  • Organization of a joint military force, with Esperanto adopted as the common language

Because his execution was brought forward ahead of schedule, the Treatise was left incomplete — only the preface and a portion of the main text survive.

Remains and Calligraphic Works

Ahn Jung-geun's remains have never been found. They are believed to have been buried near Lushun Prison, but Japanese authorities refused the family's requests for their return. Hundreds of calligraphic works he produced in prison have been passed down within Korea and abroad; some were received and kept by Japanese guards.

Key Facts

  • Period: Korean Empire (1879–1910)

  • Execution: March 26, 1910, Lushun Prison · Age 31
