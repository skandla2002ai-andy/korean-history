The Six Frontier Districts — Seo Hui's Diplomatic Triumph

During the first Khitan invasion of 993, diplomat Seo Hui negotiated Goryeo's acquisition of the Six Frontier Districts without a single battle.

Era Goryeo Topic War Topic Diplomacy Topic Territory Related China

Seo Hui's Negotiation

In 993, the Khitan general Xiao Sunning led an army reportedly 800,000 strong into Goryeo. The Khitan's ostensible demand was that Goryeo had occupied former Goguryeo territory; in substance, they were pressuring Goryeo to cut ties with the Song dynasty and submit to Khitan suzerainty. While the Goryeo court debated ceding territory north of Seogyeong (Pyongyang) to buy peace, the official Seo Hui crossed into the Khitan camp alone and negotiated directly with Xiao Sunning. Seo argued that Goryeo was the legitimate successor to Goguryeo and that the lack of diplomatic contact with Khitan was due to Jurchen tribes blocking the route — not Goryeo's choice. He pledged that Goryeo would cut ties with Song and pay tribute to Khitan.

Acquiring the Six Frontier Districts

As a result of the negotiation, the Khitan withdrew their forces and, remarkably, ceded six districts in the Gangdong region east of the Yalu River — Hwonghwa, Yongju, Tongju, Cheolju, Gwiju, and Gwakju — to Goryeo. Goryeo built fortresses there and incorporated the territory. These Six Frontier Districts (江東六州) were strategically vital ground between the Yalu and Cheongcheon rivers, serving as defensive strongpoints when the Khitan invaded a second and third time (1010 and 1018). Gang Gam-chan's annihilation of the 100,000-strong Khitan army at the Battle of Gwiju in 1019 was made possible in large part by the defensive depth these districts provided.

Significance as a Diplomatic Victory

Seo Hui's negotiation is celebrated in Korean history as the preeminent example of territorial expansion achieved through diplomacy alone, without military conflict. In a single negotiation, Goryeo pushed its northwestern border approximately 280 km northward to the Yalu River. Seo's logic — combining historical claims of succession with geopolitical realities — became a model for Goryeo diplomacy thereafter. The acquisition of the Six Frontier Districts is regarded as a defining symbol of Goryeo's pragmatic statecraft between the Khitan and Song dynasties.
