The Tripitaka Koreana — 80,000 Wooden Blocks Carved in Wartime

In 1236, while the Mongols were ravaging Goryeo, the court — sheltering as refugees on Ganghwa Island — began carving Buddhist scriptures onto more than 80,000 wooden blocks. It took sixteen years.

Era Goryeo Region Ganghwa Island Related Mongol Topic Buddhism Topic Culture

Overview

81,258 woodblocks · approx. 52 million characters · 16 years to produce · preserved at Haeinsa for 780 years

The Tripitaka Koreana (八萬大藏經, Palman Daejanggyeong) is a woodblock edition of the Buddhist canon produced over sixteen years from 1236 to 1251, during the reign of King Gojong of Goryeo. It is currently stored in the Janggyeong Panjeon (Scripture Storage Hall) at Haeinsa Temple in Hapcheon, South Gyeongsang Province, and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995 and a UNESCO Memory of the World in 2007.

Background

The Mongols invaded Goryeo for the first time in 1231. The Goryeo court chose to resist and moved the capital to Ganghwa Island in 1232. That same year, during the second Mongol invasion, the woodblocks of the first Tripitaka (Chojo Daejanggyeong), produced in 1011, were destroyed by fire. The court decided to re-carve the woodblock canon — the official reason being "to repel the Mongols through the power of the Buddha."

Production and Quality

Each woodblock contains a uniform number of characters (23 lines on each side, 14 characters per line), and errors are extremely rare. The wood was primarily wild cherry and pear, soaked in seawater to remove salt, dried in the shade, then used. Each end was fitted with a frame and the surface lacquered to prevent warping. Thanks to this, the vast majority of the woodblocks have retained their original form 780-plus years later.

Transfer to Haeinsa and Preservation

The woodblocks were moved to Haeinsa in the 7th year of King Taejo of Joseon (1398). The Janggyeong Panjeon at Haeinsa is a building from the 15th century that has preserved the woodblocks using only natural ventilation — no artificial heating or cooling. The size and placement of the building's windows are designed to regulate humidity and temperature naturally.

Key Facts

  • Period: Goryeo King Gojong (produced 1236–1251)
  • Production sites: Ganghwa Island · Namhae
  • Current storage: Janggyeong Panjeon, Haeinsa, Hapcheon
  • Number of blocks: 81,258 · Characters: approx. 52 million
  • UNESCO World Heritage (1995) · Memory of the World (2007)
