Sin Chaeho — Historian and Anarchist

History is the struggle between the self (我) and the other (非我). Sin Chaeho founded nationalist historiography and died an anarchist in Lüshun prison in 1936.

Era Japanese Colonial Period Topic Independence Movement · Historiography

Overview

Sin Chaeho (申采浩, 1880–1936) was the founder of modern Korean nationalist historiography and a leading independence activist. Active as a journalist, historian, and novelist, he went into exile in China after 1919 and participated in the Provisional Government. His thought deepened into anarchism, and he drafted the "Declaration of the Korean Revolution" (Joseon Hyeongmyeong Seoneon, 1923) for the Heroic Corps (Uiyeoldan). Arrested in 1928 on charges of forging foreign currency, he died in Lüshun prison in 1936.

Historical Scholarship

In "A New Reading of History" (Doksa Sinron, 1908), Sin Chaeho advocated a nation-centered approach to historical writing. He is famous for defining history as "the record of the struggle between the self (我, a) and the other (非我, bia)." In his Joseon Sanggo-sa (Ancient History of Korea), he reconstructed Korean ancient history from the Dangun Joseon period with Goguryeo at the center. He opposed the Japanese colonial historiography's theories of heteronomy and stagnation, emphasizing the autonomy and self-determination of the Korean people.

The Declaration of the Korean Revolution

Written in 1923 for the Heroic Corps, the "Declaration of the Korean Revolution" criticized diplomatic approaches, preparationist strategies, and cultural movement theories, instead calling for direct violent revolution. It stressed "the people's direct revolution" and argued for the legitimacy of armed struggle against Japanese colonial rule.
