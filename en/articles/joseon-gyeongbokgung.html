Gyeongbokgung — The Principal Palace of the Joseon Dynasty

Built in 1395 by King Taejo Yi Seonggye, Gyeongbokgung was Joseon's foremost palace. Burned in the Imjin War and rebuilt under Regent Heungseon Daewongun.

Era Joseon Topic Culture Topic Architecture Region Seoul

Overview

Gyeongbokgung (景福宮) was built in 1395 (the 4th year of King Taejo) as the principal palace — the gyeonggung or "law palace" — of the Joseon dynasty. The name "Gyeongbok" (景福) was chosen by Jeong Do-jeon from a line in the Book of Odes: "Already drunk with wine and already full of virtue — may the lord for ten thousand years be helped to great fortune." The palace was sited with Mount Bugak behind it, Inwangsan and Naksan to left and right, and Namsan in front, following the traditional feng shui principle of backing against a mountain and facing water.

The palace's central building is Geunjeongjeon (勤政殿), the throne hall where the king held formal court with his officials. Geunjeongjeon is a double-roofed hipped-and-gabled building set atop a two-tiered stone terrace, and is one of the largest wooden structures in Joseon. Within the palace complex were also Gangnyngjeon (the king's sleeping quarters), Gyotaejeon (the queen's quarters), Sajeongjeon (the king's daily administrative hall), and Gyeonghoeru (a pavilion for royal banquets).

History

During the Imjin War of 1592, when King Seonjo fled to Uiju, the palace was burned by enraged commoners. It remained unrestored for over 270 years until 1865 (the 2nd year of King Gojong), when Regent Heungseon Daewongun undertook a reconstruction project to reassert royal authority. Timber was requisitioned from across the country, and large sums were raised through donations (known as wonnap-jeon), completing the rebuilding in 1868. The reconstructed palace comprised over 330 buildings.

During the Japanese colonial period the palace was severely damaged. In 1915, under the pretext of holding the Joseon Industrial Exposition, most of the buildings were demolished and the Government-General building was erected in front of Geunjeongjeon. After liberation the Government-General building continued to serve as the Central Government Hall before being demolished in 1996. From 1990 the government undertook a restoration project, progressively restoring the Heungnyemun, Taeweonjeon, and East Palace precincts. The palace is designated as Historic Site No. 117.

Historical Significance

Gyeongbokgung is a space where the politics, culture, and arts of the Joseon dynasty converge, representing the pinnacle of traditional Korean architecture. Several buildings, including Geunjeongjeon, are designated as National Treasures or Treasures, and the Gyeonghoeru Pond preserves the beauty of traditional Korean landscape design. Today Gyeongbokgung is visited by millions of people each year and stands as a symbol of Korea's historical identity.
