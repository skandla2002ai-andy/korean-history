The History of Kimchi — 2,000 Years of Fermentation Before Chili Peppers

Today's red kimchi is only 300 years old. For the 1,700 years before that, kimchi was simply salted vegetables.

Topic Food & Culture Era Joseon

Overview

Kimchi is a traditional Korean fermented food made from vegetables salted and seasoned with spices. In 2013, UNESCO inscribed "Kimjang, making and sharing kimchi" on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Koreans today consume approximately 1.8 million tonnes of kimchi annually.

Kimchi Before Chili Peppers

Chili peppers arrived in Korea after the Imjin War (1592–1598), most likely via Japan. The red chili-seasoned kimchi familiar today did not become widespread until the 18th century. Before that, kimchi (called chimchae, 沈菜, meaning "submerged vegetables") was a white, salted preparation flavored with garlic and ginger. Even today, varieties such as baek-kimchi (white kimchi) and nabak-kimchi preserve traces of this pre-chili form.

The Science of Fermentation

The key to kimchi fermentation is lactic acid bacteria (Lactobacillus). Salt draws moisture out of the vegetables; in the resulting anaerobic environment, lactic acid bacteria multiply and produce lactic acid, which both preserves the kimchi and develops its flavor. The process generates vitamin C, B vitamins, and probiotics. Kimchi served as a vital nutritional supplement during the winter months when fresh vegetables were scarce.

Key Facts

Fermentation history: approximately 2,000 years · Chili pepper introduction: post-Imjin War (late 16th–early 17th century) · Red kimchi established: 18th century · UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: 2013 · Annual consumption: approximately 1.8 million tonnes
