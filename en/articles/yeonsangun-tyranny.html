Yeonsangun's Tyranny — Two Purges and Madness

The Muosahwa and Gapjasahwa purges. Yeonsangun's reign of terror and the first royal coup in Joseon history.

Era Joseon Topic Person Topic Politics

Overview

Yeonsangun (燕山君, r. 1494–1506) was the tenth king of Joseon. During his reign he unleashed two purges of the literati, ruled through terror, and was ultimately deposed in a coup. Born the eldest son of King Seongjong, he was profoundly shaped—psychologically and politically—by the fact that his biological mother, Queen Consort Yun, had been demoted and forced to drink poison. He used the Muosahwa purge of 1498 and the Gapjasahwa purge of 1504 to liquidate scores of Sarim and Hungu officials. In his final years he converted Sungkyunkwan into a pleasure house, tore down commoners' homes to expand his hunting grounds, and even banned the use of Hangul after anonymous critiques of his rule began circulating. In 1506 a coup led by Pak Weonjong, Seong Huian, and Yu Sunjeong deposed him and sent him into exile on Ganghwa Island (later Gyodong Island), where he died of illness before the year was out.

The Muosahwa and Gapjasahwa Purges

The Muosahwa purge of 1498 (the fourth year of Yeonsangun's reign) was triggered when Kim Jongjik's disciple Kim Iljson included his teacher's essay "Dirge for King Yi" (弔義帝文) in the official court history. Because the essay was interpreted as a veiled criticism of King Sejo's usurpation of the throne, Yeonsangun seized on it as a pretext to execute and exile numerous Sarim scholars. Kim Jongjik himself, already dead, was subjected to posthumous desecration of his corpse. In character, the Muosahwa was largely a political purge in which the Hungu faction used Yeonsangun to eliminate their Sarim rivals.

The Gapjasahwa purge of 1504 (the tenth year of his reign) was different in nature. Yeonsangun took personal revenge on those who had been involved in his mother's demotion and execution, conducting a sweeping purge that spared neither Hungu nor Sarim. Even the already-deceased royal concubines Eom and Jeong were posthumously executed, and veteran senior ministers who had voted to demote Queen Consort Yun were made to suffer. The Gapjasahwa was an eruption of private vengeance and madness into state violence.

The Deepening Tyranny and the Jungjong Coup

After the purges, Yeonsangun's oppression only intensified. He repurposed Sungkyunkwan as an entertainment venue, filled the palace with kisaeng courtesans, and when anonymous Hangul critiques of his rule appeared, banned the use of Hangul altogether. He razed commoners' homes to extend his hunting grounds, losing virtually all popular support. In September 1506 (by the lunar calendar), Pak Weonjong, Seong Huian, and Yu Sunjeong launched a coup, deposed Yeonsangun, and exiled him to Ganghwa Island (later Gyodong Island). He died of illness at his place of exile later that year. It was the first coup of its kind in Joseon history.

Historical Significance

Paradoxically, Yeonsangun's tyranny contributed to the development of Sarim-centered politics in Joseon. Although the Sarim faction suffered enormous losses in the two purges, after the Jungjong coup they took root in local communities across the country, gradually built their strength, and emerged as the dominant force at court from the mid-sixteenth century onward. Yeonsangun remains in history as a cautionary example of what the abuse of royal power can bring about.
