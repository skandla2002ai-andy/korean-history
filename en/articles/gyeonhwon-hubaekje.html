Gyeonhwon — The General Who Founded Later Baekje

From Silla soldier to king of Later Baekje, Gyeonhwon was ousted by his own son and surrendered to his lifelong rival Wang Geon.

Era Unified Silla Topic People Topic War Topic Politics Region Jeolla

The Rise of Regional Warlords in Late Silla

Gyeonhwon (867–936) was the founder of Later Baekje (Hubaekje) during the Later Three Kingdoms period. Born into a farming family in Sangju, he rose through the ranks of the Silla military and distinguished himself in the southwestern coastal defense forces. In the late 9th century, as the Silla royal court fell into extreme weakness and provincial strongmen grew independently powerful, Gyeonhwon seized the moment to strike out on his own.

Founding and Expansion of Later Baekje

In 900, Gyeonhwon proclaimed the founding of Later Baekje with Wansanju (present-day Jeonju) as his capital. He invoked the cause of avenging the defeat of Baekje's King Uija, rallying support from the descendants of the old Baekje population. At its peak, Later Baekje controlled most of the southwestern Korean Peninsula. In 927, Gyeonhwon launched a stunning raid on Silla's capital, Gyeongju, forcing King Gyeongae to take his own life and installing King Gyeongsun in his place — an event that shook even the symbolic authority of the Silla court.

Internal Strife and a Tragic End

Gyeonhwon's downfall came not from outside enemies but from within his own family. When he designated his fourth son Geumgang as heir, his eldest son Singeom imprisoned his father in Geumsansa Temple in 930 and seized the throne. Gyeonhwon escaped and submitted himself — in a profound humiliation — to his lifelong rival Wang Geon of Goryeo. In 936, when Wang Geon's forces crushed Later Baekje at the Battle of Illicheon, Gyeonhwon stood on Wang Geon's side against his own son Singeom. He died later that year at Hwangsan.

Historical Significance

Gyeonhwon was a figure who broke through the rigid bone-rank restrictions of Silla society through sheer ability. His founding of Later Baekje was not mere rebellion but a genuine attempt to build a new state grounded in the identity of the former Baekje territories. Though he was ultimately defeated, his existence ensured that Goryeo faced its most formidable rival in the unification of the Later Three Kingdoms.
