Gang Gam-chan and the Battle of Gwiju — Annihilating 100,000 Khitan

In 1019, Gang Gam-chan annihilated a 100,000-strong Khitan army at Gwiju — Goryeo's greatest military victory.

Era Goryeo Topic War Topic Person Related China

Overview

The Battle of Gwiju (龜州大捷) was fought in the second lunar month of 1019 (the 10th year of King Hyeonjong), when Goryeo's great general Gang Gam-chan (姜邯贊, 948–1031) virtually annihilated a 100,000-strong Khitan army under General Xiao Paipress at Gwiju (present-day Guseong, North Pyeongan Province). It was the third Khitan invasion, following those of 993 and 1010; the victory secured Goryeo's independent standing in the East Asian international order.

Gang Gam-chan, then 72 years old, was appointed Supreme Commander and led 208,000 troops. Ranked alongside the Battle of Salsu and the Battle of Haengju as one of Korea's Three Great Battles, the Battle of Gwiju represents the apex of Goryeo's military power.

The Third Khitan Invasion and Strategic Background

After ceding the Six Frontier Districts to Goryeo following the 993 invasion, the Khitan (Liao dynasty) persistently demanded their return. The second invasion (1010) sacked the capital Gaekyeong and drove King Hyeonjong into exile in the south, but the Khitan still failed to achieve full submission. After eleven unsuccessful diplomatic missions demanding the return of the Six Frontier Districts, the Khitan launched the third invasion in December 1018, sending Xiao Paipress with 100,000 troops.

Gang Gam-chan waited for the moment the Khitan crossed the Samgyocheon stream at Hwonghwajin. He had dammed the river upstream using cowhide bags; when the Khitan cavalry were halfway across, he released the flood, throwing the horsemen into chaos and neutralizing their shock power. The Khitan army pressed on to near the capital Gaekyeong, but plagued by supply shortages and relentless Goryeo guerrilla attacks, they decided to retreat.

The Battle and Its Outcome

In the second lunar month of 1019, Gang Gam-chan encircled and annihilated the retreating Khitan army on the plains of Gwiju. The Goryeosa records that "only a few thousand survived to return home"; modern scholars estimate that between 70,000 and 80,000 of the 100,000 Khitan troops were killed or captured. Xiao Paipress escaped with his life but faced severe censure from the Khitan emperor upon his return.

After the war, Goryeo and the Khitan resumed peace negotiations from 1020 onward. The Khitan made no further demands for the Six Frontier Districts, and Goryeo was effectively free from the threat of Khitan aggression. So great was Gang Gam-chan's triumph that King Hyeonjong personally came out to Yeongpa Station to welcome him home.

Historical Significance

The Battle of Gwiju was more than a military victory — it was a defining symbol of Goryeo's sovereignty. In its aftermath, Goryeo secured an independent diplomatic space among Song, Khitan, and the Jurchen tribes, and developed a new confidence in its ability to contend as an equal with great powers. A shrine to Gang Gam-chan was later built at Nakseongdae (落星垈, in present-day Gwanak-gu, Seoul), and he is remembered to this day as an icon of loyalty and strategic brilliance.
