History of Hanbok — Two Thousand Years of Korean Dress

From the narrow sleeves of the Three Kingdoms period to the skirts and jeogori of Joseon: two thousand years of hanbok's evolution.

Era Three Kingdoms Topic Culture Topic History

Overview

Hanbok is the traditional dress of the Korean Peninsula, developed over many centuries in response to the local climate, way of life, and aesthetic sensibilities. Starting from the Three Kingdoms period dress characterized by narrow sleeves and straight silhouettes, the basic forms — jeogori (jacket), chima (skirt), baji (trousers), and po (overcoat) — were established through the Goryeo and Joseon periods. During the Joseon dynasty, clothing governed by Confucian ritual became a social marker of status, gender, and occasion, and the classic forms we call "hanbok" today were fully established in this era.

Evolution Through the Ages

Goguryeo tomb murals from the Three Kingdoms period vividly depict men and women wearing jeogori, baji, and chima. The dress of this era was practical and active in structure, reflecting the influence of northern equestrian culture. During the Unified Silla period, Tang Chinese influence brought wide-sleeved overcoats into fashion among the aristocracy.

In the Goryeo period, Mongol-style dress entered upper-class fashion through the influence of the Yuan dynasty; jeogori became shorter and the waistline of the chima rose higher. During the Joseon dynasty, clothing was rigorously codified according to Neo-Confucian ritual principles. Colors, materials, and adornments differed by social rank: the literati wore white overcoats (dopo) and horsehair hats (gat), while commoners wore plain cotton overcoats (durumagi). Women's jeogori grew progressively shorter over the course of the late Joseon period.

Historical Significance

Hanbok is a cultural heritage that reflects a complex interplay of Korea's natural environment, way of life, and aesthetic taste. Following the opening of ports in the late 19th century, Western-style dress was introduced and hanbok gradually retreated from everyday wear, but it has continued to be worn at ceremonial occasions such as Lunar New Year, weddings, and first-birthday celebrations. In recent years, the popularity of "everyday hanbok" and contemporary hanbok design has grown, and international interest has risen alongside the spread of Korean popular culture.
