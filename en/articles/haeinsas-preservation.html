Haeinsa and the Tripitaka Koreana — 81,258 Woodblocks That Did Not Warp

Made between 1236 and 1251: 81,258 wooden printing blocks, 52 million characters. Seven hundred and fifty years later, they have not warped.

Era Goryeo Topic Buddhism & Culture

Overview

The Tripitaka Koreana (Palman Daejanggyeong, or Goryeo Daejanggyeong) is a complete set of Buddhist scriptures carved onto 81,258 wooden printing blocks during the reign of King Gojong of Goryeo (1236–1251), commissioned to invoke the power of the Buddha against the Mongol invasions. The blocks are housed in Janggyeongpanjeon (the Depositories for the Tripitaka Koreana) at Haeinsa Temple in Hapcheon, South Gyeongsang Province. The Janggyeongpanjeon was designated a UNESCO World Cultural Heritage site in 1995, and the Tripitaka itself was inscribed on the UNESCO Memory of the World Register in 2007.

The Secret of 750 Years

The blocks are made from white birch and mountain cherry trees that were soaked in seawater to infuse them with salt, then dried in the shade for three years — a specialized treatment that prevented warping and cracking. The architecture of the Janggyeongpanjeon is equally remarkable. The size of the latticed windows differs between the south and north sides, and the floor beneath the windows is layered with different soils at varying depths to enable natural ventilation and humidity control. Even by modern scientific analysis, the structure provides an optimal preservation environment.

Key Facts

Production period: 1236–1251 (15 years) · Number of blocks: 81,258 · Number of characters: approximately 52 million · Location: Janggyeongpanjeon, Haeinsa Temple · UNESCO World Cultural Heritage: 1995 · UNESCO Memory of the World: 2007
