The Jeju April 3rd Incident — The Massacre an Island Remembers

April 3, 1948. An armed uprising began on Jeju. Civilians were massacred in the crackdown. One in ten islanders died. For fifty years, no one could speak of it.

Era Modern Topic Democratization · Human Rights

Overview

The Jeju April 3rd Incident (濟州 四·三 事件) encompasses events from the shooting of civilians at a March 1st commemorative rally in 1947, through the armed uprising launched by the South Korean Labor Party's Jeju branch on April 3, 1948, the violent suppression ordered by the U.S. Military Government and the Rhee Syngman government, and the civilian massacres that continued through the immediate aftermath of the Korean War. The official death toll is estimated at 25,000 to 30,000 — approximately one-tenth of Jeju's population at the time (about 300,000).

Course of Events

On March 1, 1947, police fired on civilians at a March 1st commemoration in Jeju, killing six people. A general strike followed in protest. On April 3, 1948, armed units affiliated with the South Korean Labor Party attacked police stations and rightist organizations, triggering an uprising. The U.S. Military Government and the Rhee Syngman government ordered a harsh crackdown under the banner of "exterminating the reds." In the process, civilians with no connection to the uprising were killed on a massive scale. Dozens of midland mountain villages on Hallasan were razed to the ground.

Truth-Finding and the State Apology

For decades, April 3 was an officially taboo subject. Survivors and bereaved families were forced into silence. In 2000, the "Special Act on the Investigation of Truth and Restoration of Honor of Victims of the Jeju April 3rd Incident" was enacted. In 2003, President Roh Moo-hyun formally acknowledged the errors of state authority and offered an official apology to the people of Jeju. April 3 is now a national memorial day each year.

Key Facts

Beginning: April 3, 1948 · Estimated deaths: 25,000–30,000 · Special Act: 2000 · Official state apology: President Roh Moo-hyun, 2003 · National Memorial Day: April 3 each year
