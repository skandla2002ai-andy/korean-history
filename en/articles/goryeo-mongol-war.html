Goryeo's 39-Year Resistance Against the Mongols — A Kingdom That Held from Ganghwa Island

1231–1270. Against seven Mongol invasions, the court moved the capital to Ganghwa Island and held on for 39 years. The price was paid by the common people.

Era Goryeo Topic War Related Mongol (Yuan)

Overview

Goryeo's resistance against the Mongols (對蒙 抗爭) was a roughly 39-year struggle against seven Mongol invasions from 1231 to 1270. The Goryeo military regime (the Choe clan) moved the capital to Ganghwa Island in 1232, abandoning the mainland while continuing to resist. In 1270, the court returned to Gaegyeong (the mainland capital) and concluded a peace with the Mongols, effectively becoming a son-in-law state (駙馬國) of the Yuan empire.

The Move to Ganghwa and the Military Regime's Choice

The Mongol army's strength lay in cavalry tactics, but it was weak at sea. The Choe military regime exploited this by moving the capital to Ganghwa Island. However, this was an escape available only to the ruling class. The common people on the mainland were left exposed to Mongol plunder and slaughter, fighting on or being killed. Contemporary critics noted that while the ruling class enjoyed comfort on their island, ordinary people were dying.

The Last Stand of the Sambyeolcho

When King Wonjong returned to the mainland capital in 1270 and made peace with the Mongols, the Sambyeolcho (三別抄) under Bae Jung-son (裵仲孫) refused to accept the peace and continued resistance against the Mongols from their base at Hangpaduri (Jeju Island) until 1273. The Sambyeolcho uprising was less a spontaneous popular resistance than the last stand of remnant forces from the military regime, but their will to fight to the end left its mark on history.

Key Facts

Duration: 1231–1270 (39 years) · Mongol invasions: 7 · Move to Ganghwa: 1232 · Return to mainland capital: 1270 · Sambyeolcho resistance: 1270–1273 · Outcome: became a son-in-law state of Yuan
