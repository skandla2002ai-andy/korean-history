Japan's Suppression of the Korean Language — Take Away a People's Language and Erase a Nation

In 1938, Korean-language instruction was banned from schools. In 1942, scholars were imprisoned in the Joseoneo Hakhoe Incident. Yet Hangul survived.

Era Japanese Colonial Topic Language & Suppression Related Japan

Overview

After colonizing Korea in 1910, Japan systematically suppressed the Korean language as part of its assimilation policy. Particularly during the ethnic erasure policy of the late 1930s and early 1940s, Korea-language instruction was banned from schools, the use of Korean in newspapers and magazines was restricted, and scholars of the Joseon Language Society (Joseoneo Hakhoe) were imprisoned.

Gradual Suppression

After the annexation in 1910, Japanese-language education was intensified. Following the March First Movement of 1919, the "cultural governance" period allowed Korean-language newspapers (Dong-A Ilbo, Joseon Ilbo) to be published, though they were subject to heavy censorship. In 1938, the revised Joseon Education Ordinance abolished Korean-language education in schools. In 1942, the Joseon Language Society was persecuted by the Japanese authorities as it worked on a large-scale Korean dictionary compilation project, leading to the arrest of 33 scholars (the Joseon Language Society Incident).

The Joseon Language Society's Resistance

The Joseon Language Society (founded 1921) led the standardization of Korean through the Unified Korean Orthography (1933), the Standard Language Compilation (1936), and the Foreign Loanword Notation Rules (1940). In the 1942 incident, Yi Yunjae and Han Jing died in prison, and several other scholars were imprisoned until liberation. The dictionary manuscript, however, was found in a storage room at Seoul Station after liberation and was completed as the Keun Sajeon (Grand Dictionary, 1947–1957).

Key Facts

Korean-language instruction banned: 1938 · Joseon Language Society Incident: 1942 · Arrested: 33 people · Deaths in prison: Yi Yunjae, Han Jing · Dictionary manuscript found: 1945, Seoul Station storage · Keun Sajeon completed: 1957
