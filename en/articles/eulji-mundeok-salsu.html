Eulji Mundeok and the Battle of Salsu — Defeating 300,000 Sui

In 612 CE, Goguryeo general Eulji Mundeok annihilated a 300,000-strong Sui army at the Salsu River (Cheongcheon River) in one of history's great military victories.

The Sui Invasion of Goguryeo

In 612 CE (the 23rd year of Goguryeo King Yeongyang), Emperor Yang of Sui led a force reportedly numbering 1.13 million men against Goguryeo. Actual combat troops are estimated at around 300,000 — an extraordinary scale for East Asia at the time. Emperor Yang had spent years preparing the campaign, conscripting supplies from across the empire and digging canals to establish supply lines from as early as 611. The backdrop to the invasion included tensions built up from Goguryeo's independent foreign policy and a preemptive strike by King Yeongyang in 598. Even after the failure of Emperor Wen's first expedition, Yang launched a second, far larger campaign.

A Sui elite force of 300,000 bypassed Liaodong and was tasked with striking directly at Pyongyang. Goguryeo general Eulji Mundeok (乙支文德) boldly entered the Sui camp himself to gather intelligence. Sui commander Yu Zhongwen attempted to detain him but ultimately allowed him to leave. On his way back, Eulji sent Yu a mocking five-character poem beginning: "Your divine strategies pierce the heavens; your brilliant calculations fathom the earth" — goading the enemy general while luring him into retreat.

The Battle of Salsu — The Great Counterattack at the Cheongcheon River

Eulji Mundeok drew the Sui army deep into Goguryeo territory, bleeding them with continuous small engagements until their supply lines stretched thin and the troops were exhausted. When the Sui elite force finally abandoned its assault on Pyongyang and began to withdraw, Eulji struck. As the retreating army was crossing the Salsu River (薩水, present-day Cheongcheon River), Goguryeo forces launched a total assault. Some accounts record that floodgates upstream were opened to sweep the crossing troops away. According to the Samguk Sagi, of the 300,000 Sui soldiers, only 2,700 made it back to Liaodong alive.

The victory at Salsu decisively ended Sui's second expedition. Emperor Yang attempted further campaigns in 613 and 614, but both failed. Exhausted by the endless wars, the Sui dynasty succumbed to internal rebellion and collapsed in 618.

Historical Significance

The Battle of Salsu is considered the greatest defense against foreign invasion in Korean peninsular history. Eulji Mundeok's strategy — luring the enemy deep, cutting off supplies, wearing them down, and then delivering the killing blow at the retreat — is studied as a classic example of "scorched earth" tactics and strategic withdrawal. The battle also directly contributed to Sui's collapse, reshaping the East Asian international order. Eulji Mundeok is remembered alongside Yi Sun-sin and Gang Gam-chan as one of Korea's greatest military commanders.
