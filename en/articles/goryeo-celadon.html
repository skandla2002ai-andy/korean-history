Goryeo Celadon — The Secret of the Jade-Green Glaze

A Song-dynasty Chinese writer called it "the finest under heaven." Jade-green glaze, inlaid sanggam designs. The ceramic art that blossomed in 12th-century Goryeo.

Era Goryeo Topic Crafts · Art

Overview

Goryeo celadon (高麗靑磁) is the blue-green porcelain produced during the Goryeo period (918–1392) and is considered the high point of Korean ceramic history. Korean potters absorbed Chinese celadon techniques in the 9th and 10th centuries and refined them further, perfecting an independent jade-green glaze and the inlaid sanggam (象嵌) technique in the 12th century. The main production centers were Gangjin in South Jeolla Province and Buan in North Jeolla Province. Even during the Goryeo era, celadon was exported to China, earning international renown.

The Innovation of Sanggam Inlay

The supreme achievement of Goryeo celadon is the sanggam (inlay) technique. A design is incised into the surface of the vessel, white or red clay is pressed into the grooves, and when fired, the pattern emerges beneath the glaze. This was an original technique unknown even in China. Patterns of cranes, clouds, lotus flowers, and grapes were rendered in white and red against the jade-green ground. The masterwork most often cited is the "Celadon Maebyeong with Inlaid Cloud and Crane Design" (靑磁象嵌雲鶴文梅甁).

Decline and Legacy

Goryeo celadon production shrank and quality declined following the Mongol invasions of the 13th century. During the Joseon era, buncheong ware and white porcelain carried on the ceramic tradition in place of celadon. Today Goryeo celadon is held in major museums at home and abroad, and new pieces continue to be discovered through excavations in Gaeseong and from the seabed.

Key Facts

Golden age: 12th century · Main production sites: Gangjin (South Jeolla), Buan (North Jeolla) · Sanggam technique: a Goryeo original invention · Decline: after Mongol invasions of the 13th century · Masterwork: Celadon Maebyeong with Inlaid Cloud and Crane Design (National Treasure)
