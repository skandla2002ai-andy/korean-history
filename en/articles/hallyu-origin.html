The Origins of Hallyu — How the IMF Crisis Gave Birth to K-Pop

The 1997 financial crisis. Cultivating the cultural industry became a national strategy. Those seeds became BTS and Bong Joon-ho twenty years later.

Era Modern Topic Culture · Hallyu

Overview

Hallyu (韓流, Korean Wave) is the worldwide spread of Korean popular culture — dramas, music, film, webtoons, food, and more — that began in Asia from the late 1990s. The romanization "Hallyu" is used internationally, and the phenomenon has spawned the "K-" brand umbrella: K-pop, K-drama, K-beauty, and beyond.

The IMF Financial Crisis and the Cultural Industry

The 1997 Asian financial crisis forced Korea to accept an IMF bailout, delivering a severe shock to the economy. The Kim Dae-jung government designated the cultural industry as the next engine of growth, enacted the "Basic Act on Cultural Industry Promotion" (1999), and began large-scale investment in film, music, and gaming. During this period, entertainment companies such as SM Entertainment built systematic idol training systems.

The Catalysts of the Wave

The drama Winter Sonata (2002) sparked an explosive response in Japan, igniting the Korean Wave across Asia. In 2012, Psy's "Gangnam Style" exceeded one billion views on YouTube. BTS dominated the US Billboard charts from 2018 onward. Director Bong Joon-ho's Parasite won the Academy Award for Best Picture in 2020.

Key Facts

Origins: late 1990s (Asian drama wave) · Basic Act on Cultural Industry Promotion: 1999 · Winter Sonata: 2002 · Gangnam Style: 2012 · BTS Billboard No. 1: 2020 · Parasite Academy Award: 2020
