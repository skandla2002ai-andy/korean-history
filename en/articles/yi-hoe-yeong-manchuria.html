Yi Hoe-yeong — The Aristocrat Who Sold Everything for Independence

Six brothers who devoted their entire fortune to the independence movement. Yi Hoe-yeong and the founding of Sinheung Military Academy in Manchuria.

Era Japanese Colonial Period Topic Person Topic Independence Movement Related Japan Related China

A Nobleman Who Poured His Entire Fortune into Independence

Yi Hoe-yeong (1867–1932) came from the Gyeongju Yi clan, one of the most illustrious aristocratic families in Joseon. Immediately after Japan's annexation of Korea in 1910, he led his entire family into exile in Manchuria and dedicated his life to the independence movement. Together with his five brothers, he liquidated the family's entire fortune—more than 400,000 won in the currency of the day, equivalent to tens of billions of won in present value—and channeled every penny into funding the resistance.

Founding of Sinheung Military Academy

In 1911, Yi Hoe-yeong joined Yi Sang-nyong, Yi Dong-nyeong, and others to establish the Gyeonghaksa (耕學社) farming-and-learning cooperative at Samwonbo in the Seogando region of Manchuria, and founded the Sinheung Lecture School, which later became the Sinheung Military Academy (신흥무관학교). The school trained more than 3,500 independence army officers; the commanders who fought in major engagements such as the Battle of Cheongsanri were graduates of this institution. Yi Hoe-yeong himself embraced anarchism and pursued a form of resistance that operated outside formal organizational structures.

Tragic End and the Sacrifices of His Family

After the Manchurian Incident of 1932 worsened conditions in the region, Yi Hoe-yeong crossed into Manchuria to organize armed anti-Japanese forces. He was captured by the Japanese police and died under brutal torture at Lüshun Prison. He was 66 years old. Many of his five brothers perished in exile from starvation and disease, and his descendants lived in poverty for many years.

Historical Significance

Yi Hoe-yeong's life represents the extreme of resistance chosen by someone at the very top of the privilege structure who gave up everything. The Sinheung Military Academy he founded was the cradle of the independence army's armed struggle, and his anarchist approach to the independence movement stands as a striking example of the ideological diversity within Korea's resistance tradition.
