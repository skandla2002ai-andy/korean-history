Admiral Yi Sun-sin and the Battle of Myeongnyang — 12 Ships vs. 333

Falsely accused and dismissed, tortured, left with only 12 ships after the navy was decimated — Yi Sun-sin faced 333 Japanese warships. And won.

Era Joseon Topic Imjin War Topic People Related Japan

Dismissal and Reinstatement

In January 1597, Yi Sun-sin was dismissed through political intrigue, imprisoned, and tortured. On the brink of execution, he was saved by the intercession of State Councilor Jeong Tak and reinstated as a common soldier. That July, the Joseon navy under Won Gyun was annihilated at the Battle of Chilchonryang. King Seonjo urgently reappointed Yi Sun-sin as Supreme Naval Commander of the Three Provinces. The fleet Yi inherited numbered just 12 ships.

When Seonjo ordered him to "dissolve the navy and join the land forces," Yi refused and submitted a memorial to the throne.

"I still have twelve ships. If we fight to the death, we can yet prevail."
今臣戰船尙有十二 出死力拒戰則猶可爲也

Battle of Myeongnyang (September 16, 1597)

Yi Sun-sin chose the Uldolmok strait, known for its fierce tidal currents, as his battlefield. He timed the engagement to coincide with currents favorable to his side, blocking the narrow channel entrance and forcing the Japanese fleet to approach one vessel at a time. The enemy could not bring their superior numbers to bear in the constricted waterway.

In his war diary Nanjung Ilgi, the entry for September 15 — the day before battle — reads:

必死卽生 必生卽死
He who seeks death will live; he who seeks life will die.

The result: 31 Japanese warships sunk or heavily damaged, and the Japanese fleet withdrew. Joseon casualties were minimal.

Battle of Noryang and Death in Battle

In 1598, Toyotomi Hideyoshi died and Japanese forces began withdrawing. Yi Sun-sin intercepted the retreating Japanese fleet at Noryang in one final naval battle. In the fighting, he was struck by an enemy bullet and killed. His reported last words were: "The battle is at its peak — do not announce my death."

Key Facts

  • Battle of Myeongnyang: September 16, 1597, Uldolmok strait
  • Forces: Joseon 12 ships vs. Japanese 333 ships
  • Yi Sun-sin's death: November 1598, Battle of Noryang
  • Nanjung Ilgi (War Diary): UNESCO Memory of the World (2013)
