Park Chung-hee and Industrialization — The Miracle on the Han River and the Cost of Dictatorship

1961–1979. GDP per capita rose from $82 to $1,644. But behind that stood the Yusin constitution and the suppression of democracy.

Era Modern Topic Politics · Economy Topic Figures

Overview

Park Chung-hee (朴正熙, 1917–1979) seized power through the military coup of May 16, 1961, and ruled for 18 years until his assassination on October 26, 1979. Through Five-Year Economic Development Plans, he pursued export-led industrialization that transformed Korea from an agrarian society into an industrial one. However, in 1972 he proclaimed the Yusin Constitution and entrenched a long-term authoritarian regime.

Economic Achievements

When Park took power in 1961, GDP per capita was approximately $82 — among the world's poorest nations. By the time of his death in 1979, it had risen roughly 20-fold to about $1,644. Major milestones include the Gyeongbu Expressway (1970), POSCO steel mill (1973), and the achievement of $10 billion in exports (1977). Annual GDP growth averaged approximately 9% during this period.

The Cost of Dictatorship

The Yusin (維新) Constitution of 1972 abolished direct presidential elections, replacing them with an indirect system through the National Conference for Unification, and granted the president emergency powers and the authority to dissolve the National Assembly. Emergency Decrees (Nos. 1–9) banned any criticism of the constitution. Countless democracy activists were imprisoned, tortured, and executed during this era. The YH incident (1979) and the Busan–Masan Uprising (1979) followed in quick succession, and on October 26, Park was shot and killed by Kim Jae-gyu, director of the Korean Central Intelligence Agency.

Key Facts

Came to power: May 16, 1961 (coup) · Yusin Constitution: 1972 · Gyeongbu Expressway: 1970 · POSCO: 1973 · Death: October 26, 1979 (shot by Kim Jae-gyu)
