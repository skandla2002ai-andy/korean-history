Bak Eun-sik — History Is the Soul of the Nation (Hanguk Tongsa)

Bak Eun-sik, who wrote the Hanguk Tongsa under Japanese rule. His conviction: "A nation may perish, but its history cannot."

Era Japanese Colonial Period Topic People Topic Independence Movement Topic History Related Japan

An Independence Activist Who Fought Through History

Bak Eun-sik (1859–1925) was a historian, journalist, and independence activist who was active from the late Joseon period through the Japanese colonial era — a pioneer of Korean nationalist historiography. Born in Hwangju, Hwanghae Province, he received a Neo-Confucian education but embraced the ideas of the Enlightenment movement and devoted himself to journalism and education. After going into exile following the loss of national sovereignty in 1910, he dedicated the rest of his life to preserving the Korean national spirit through historical writing.

Hanguk Tongsa and Hanguk Dongnip Undong Ji Hyeolsa

Bak Eun-sik's major works are Hanguk Tongsa (韓國痛史, A Painful History of Korea), published in Shanghai in 1915, and Hanguk Dongnip Undong Ji Hyeolsa (韓國獨立運動之血史, A Bloody History of the Korean Independence Movement), published in 1920. Hanguk Tongsa is the first modern historical work to systematically document the process of Japanese aggression from the late Joseon period, famous for the declaration: "A nation may perish, but its history cannot (國可滅 史不可滅)." Hanguk Dongnip Undong Ji Hyeolsa vividly recorded the full scope of the independence movement, including the March First Movement.

President of the Provisional Government

Bak Eun-sik served as the second President of the Korean Provisional Government in 1925. At the time, however, the Provisional Government was in an extremely difficult situation due to internal strife and financial crisis. Shortly after taking office, he revised the constitution to transition from a presidential system to a State Council (Gungmuyeong) system, contributing to the establishment of a collective leadership structure. He died in Shanghai in November of that same year.

Historical Significance

Bak Eun-sik viewed history as a vessel containing the national spirit (魂). His historical writing was a spiritual weapon for preserving Korean identity against Japanese colonial rule. As the founder of modern nationalist historiography, his achievements are regarded as the roots of Korean historical scholarship today.
