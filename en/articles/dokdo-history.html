The History of Dokdo — From 512 to 1952

Korean territory since Silla subjugated Usan-guk in 512 CE. Japan declared its "incorporation" in 1905; Korea proclaimed effective control in 1952.

Topic Territory · Diplomacy Era Modern & Contemporary Related Japan

Overview

Dokdo (獨島) is South Korean territory belonging to Ulleung-eup, Ulleung County, North Gyeongsang Province. It consists of two main islands — East Islet and West Islet — and 89 associated rocks and reefs. Since 512 CE, when Silla general Yi Sabu subjugated Usan-guk (comprising Ulleungdo and Dokdo), Korea has administered the islands. Today Korea exercises effective control, though Japan claims sovereignty.

Historical Evidence

Key Korean historical sources: Silla's subjugation of Usan-guk in 512 (Samguk Sagi) · The entry for Usan-do (Dokdo) in the Sejong Sillok Jiriji (1454) · An Yong-bok's voyage to Japan and his protest in 1696 · The explicit mention of Dokdo in Imperial Korean Edict No. 41 (1900). Japan's argument: Japan claims "occupation of terra nullius" based on the Shimane Prefecture incorporation notice of 1905.

20th-Century Developments

During the Russo-Japanese War, Japan incorporated Dokdo via Shimane Prefecture notice in 1905. After Japan's defeat in 1945, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers Instruction Note (SCAPIN) 677 classified Dokdo under Korean jurisdiction. In 1952, President Rhee Syngman proclaimed the "Peace Line," including Dokdo within Korean-controlled waters. Since then, Korean police have been stationed on the island, maintaining effective control.

Key Facts

Silla subjugation: 512 CE · Imperial Korean Edict No. 41: 1900 · Japanese incorporation notice: 1905 · SCAPIN 677 (Korean jurisdiction): 1946 · Rhee's Peace Line: 1952 · Present: effective Korean control
