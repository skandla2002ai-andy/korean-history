The Donghak Peasant Movement — Farmers Who Raised the Banner of "Save the Nation, Protect the People"

1894. The peasant army led by Jeon Bong-jun rose up crying "People are Heaven." The outcome was the Sino-Japanese War.

Era Late Joseon Topic Popular Movement Related Japan Related China (Qing)

Overview

The Donghak Peasant Movement (東學農民運動, 1894) was a peasant uprising led by Jeon Bong-jun (全琫準, 1855–1895) and others, grounded in the Donghak (Eastern Learning) philosophy. Its slogans — "boguk anmin" (protect the nation, comfort the people) and "jepok gumin" (remove tyranny, save the people) — expressed resistance to the contradictions of the Joseon feudal system and foreign encroachment. The first uprising (January 1894) reached its peak with the capture of Jeonju Fortress, and when the Joseon government requested Qing troops, it became the spark that ignited the Sino-Japanese War.

The First Uprising and the Jeonju Treaty

In January 1894, the uprising began in resistance to the extortion of Gobu County Magistrate Jo Byeong-gap. The peasant army defeated government forces at Hwangtohyeon (April 7) and Hwangryongchon, and captured Jeonju Fortress on May 31. Feeling threatened, the Joseon government called for Qing troops. In response, the Donghak side agreed to the Jeonju Treaty, which established local self-governance organs (jipgangso), and voluntarily disbanded.

The Second Uprising and the Battle of Ugeumchi

As Japan gained the upper hand in the Sino-Japanese War and Japanese troops occupied Gyeongbokgung Palace (July 23), Jeon Bong-jun raised the anti-Japanese banner for a second uprising. However, at the Battle of Ugeumchi (牛禁峙) in November 1894 the peasant army was crushed by Japan's modern weaponry. Jeon was arrested in December and executed the following year.

Key Facts

Year: 1894 · Leader: Jeon Bong-jun · First uprising: capture of Jeonju Fortress · Jipgangso established after the Jeonju Treaty · Second uprising: defeated at Battle of Ugeumchi · Triggered the Sino-Japanese War
