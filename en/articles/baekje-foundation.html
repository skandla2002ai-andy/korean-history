King Onjo and the Founding of Baekje — The Brothers' Parting of Ways

18 BCE. Jumong's two sons, Biryu and Onjo, each founded kingdoms — at Michuhol and Wiryeseong. The elder's kingdom fell; the younger's lasted 700 years.

Era Three Kingdoms Topic Founding · History Region Chungcheong · Han River

Overview

Baekje (百濟) was founded in 18 BCE by King Onjo in the area of present-day Seoul (Wiryeseong). Onjo and Biryu, said to be sons of Jumong, founder of Goguryeo, traveled south and each established their own kingdom. Biryu's kingdom at Michuhol (present-day Incheon) soon declined, and its people were absorbed into Onjo's Baekje.

Founding and Growth

Onjo established his capital at Wiryeseong and obtained a grant of land from the Mahan confederacy to lay the kingdom's foundations. Baekje grew on the strength of the fertile farmland of the Han River basin and the advantages of river transport. It reached its golden age under King Geunchogo (r. 346–375 CE), who attacked Goguryeo's Pyongyang fortress and conducted exchanges with China's Liaodong region and Japan's Kyushu.

Seven Hundred Years of History

Baekje endured for approximately 678 years from 18 BCE until its fall to the Silla–Tang alliance in 660 CE. It moved its capital three times — from Hanseong (Seoul) to Ungjin (Gongju) and then to Sabi (Buyeo) — weathering many rises and falls. Among the Three Kingdoms, Baekje demonstrated the most refined artistic sensibility and served as a bridge transmitting Buddhism and advanced culture to Japan.

Key Facts

Founded: 18 BCE · Founder: King Onjo · Capital changes: Hanseong → Ungjin (475 CE) → Sabi (538 CE) · Golden age: King Geunchogo (346–375 CE) · Fall: 660 CE (Silla–Tang alliance)
