Goguryeo's Gaema Cavalry — The Mightiest Heavy Cavalry in East Asia

Goguryeo's iron-clad gaema cavalry. The heavy cavalry tactics that dominated East Asian battlefields in the 5th century.

Era Three Kingdoms Period Topic Military Topic Culture Related China Region Pyongyang

Overview

Gaema musa (鎧馬武士) were the heavy cavalry (重裝騎兵) of Goguryeo — the most formidable fighting force of their time, with both rider and horse fully encased in iron armor. "Gaema" means an armored horse; Chinese historical sources record them as "gaema jisa" (鎧馬之士) or "iron-armored cavalry" (鐵甲騎兵). The rider wore full-body armor including a helmet, while the horse was protected by face armor (mamyeongap), neck armor (magyeongap), and body armor (mashin'gap).

The gaema cavalry's primary weapon was the long lance. In a charge they formed a tight formation to break through enemy infantry squares. Goguryeo tomb murals from the era of King Gwanggaeto — including Anak Tomb No. 3 — vividly depict both horse and rider covered in armor from head to hoof, providing concrete evidence of the gaema cavalry in action.

Background and Development

Goguryeo dominated the vast Manchurian plains and developed a sophisticated equestrian culture. In the late 4th century, King Gwanggaeto (r. 391–412) used the gaema cavalry as his core strike force to conquer Liaodong and Liaoxi, and to extend his reach south to Baekje and Silla and north to the Sushen (predecessors of the Jurchen). In 400 CE, when Silla appealed for help against a Wa (Japanese) invasion, Gwanggaeto dispatched an army of 50,000 infantry and gaema cavalry that advanced as far as the lower Nakdong River and drove out the Wa forces. This is recorded on the Gwanggaeto Stele.

Under King Jangsu (r. 413–491) the gaema cavalry was further organized and expanded. In 475 Jangsu captured Baekje's capital Hanseong and killed King Gaero, making full use of the charging power of the gaema cavalry. During Goguryeo's peak in the 5th and 6th centuries the gaema cavalry maintained forces numbering in the hundreds of thousands, playing a pivotal role in repelling invasions by Sui and Tang. Even at the Battle of Salsu in 612, Goguryeo's cavalry contributed decisively to reversing the tide of battle.

Historical Significance

The gaema cavalry represents a complete form of heavy cavalry tactics in East Asian military history. Goguryeo's gaema tactics influenced cavalry deployment in Balhae and Goryeo, and contributed to the recognition of the tactical value of heavy cavalry across East Asia. There is also an assessment that Tang China's experience fighting Goguryeo became a catalyst for developing its own cavalry tactics.

The depictions of gaema cavalry in Goguryeo tomb murals are a precious cultural heritage demonstrating the excellence of ancient Korean military culture. Cavalry-related paintings found in Anak Tomb No. 3, Deokheung-ri Tomb, and Samsilchong are part of the Goguryeo Tomb Complex — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — transmitting to posterity the reality of the 5th century's most powerful military force in East Asia.
