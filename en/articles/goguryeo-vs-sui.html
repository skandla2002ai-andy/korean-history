Battle of Salsu — Eulji Mundeok Defeats the Sui Army of 1.13 Million

612 CE. Emperor Yang of Sui's army of 1.13 million. Caught in Eulji Mundeok's luring tactics at the Salsu River (Cheongcheon River), the Sui forces were annihilated. Only 2,700 made it back alive.

Era Three Kingdoms Period Topic War & Diplomacy Related China (Sui)

Overview

The Battle of Salsu (薩水大捷, 612 CE) was the decisive defeat of Sui China's second invasion by Goguryeo General Eulji Mundeok (乙支文德) at the Salsu River (present-day Cheongcheon River). According to the Samguk Sagi, of the Sui expeditionary force of 305,000, only 2,700 survived to return home. The shock of this defeat accelerated the rapid decline of the Sui dynasty.

Eulji Mundeok's Strategy

Eulji Mundeok employed a scorched-earth (清野) strategy of luring the enemy deep into Goguryeo territory. As the Sui expeditionary force advanced to near Pyongyang, he bought time through feigned surrender negotiations. When the Sui army, its supply lines severed, began to retreat, he had dammed the Salsu River and released the waters at the moment the massive army was crossing — drowning large numbers of troops. He then pursued the retreating forces and annihilated them.

Historical Significance

After this defeat the Sui dynasty's power declined sharply and it was overthrown by the Tang dynasty in 618. The Battle of Salsu transformed the international order of East Asia. Eulji Mundeok is also famous for sending a self-confident five-character verse poem (五言詩) to a Sui commander before the battle.

Key Facts

Year: 612 CE · Location: Salsu (Cheongcheon River) · Goguryeo commander: Eulji Mundeok · Sui invasion force: approximately 1.13 million (expeditionary force: 305,000) · Sui survivors: 2,700 (Samguk Sagi) · Outcome: rapid decline and fall of Sui (618 CE)
