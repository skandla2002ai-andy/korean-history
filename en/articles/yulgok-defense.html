Yulgok's Proposal to Train 100,000 Soldiers — The Warning Was There

Eight years before the Imjin War, Yi I argued that 100,000 soldiers must be trained. Nobody listened. In 1592 the Japanese came.

Era Joseon Topic Defense · Thought

Overview

Yi I (李珥, 1536–1584, pen name Yulgok) was the foremost Confucian scholar and statesman of mid-Joseon. In 1583, while serving as Minister of War under King Seonjo, he famously advanced what is known as the "Proposal to Train 100,000 Soldiers" (十萬養兵說): he argued that a great calamity would strike within ten years and that Joseon must immediately raise an army of 100,000 men. The court rejected the idea, and Yi I died in 1584. Eight years later the Imjin War began.

Source of the Proposal

The sources for the 100,000-soldier proposal are records compiled after Yi I's death. It appears in the Revised Annals of King Seonjo and in the testimonies of his disciples. Some historians question the reliability of these accounts, pointing out that no direct record of the proposal exists in Yi I's own collected works, the Yulgok Jeonseo—the root of the ongoing scholarly debate.

Yi I's Other Achievements

Regardless of the historicity of the 100,000-soldier proposal, Yi I stands among the greatest figures in Joseon's intellectual history. Together with Yi Hwang (Toegye), he forms one of the two towering pillars of Joseon Neo-Confucianism. Throughout his career he consistently advocated for practical reforms in education, military administration, and taxation. His portrait appears on the 5,000-won banknote.

Key Facts

Dates: 1536–1584 · Proposal to train 100,000 soldiers: 1583 (source debated) · Imjin War: 1592 · Banknote: 5,000-won bill · Mother: Sin Saimdang
