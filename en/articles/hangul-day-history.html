History of Hangul Day — Made a Holiday, Taken Away, Restored

First created in 1926, suppressed by Japan, made a national holiday after liberation, removed from the holiday calendar in 1991, and restored in 2013 — the turbulent history of Hangul Day.

Era Modern Topic Hangul & Holidays

Overview

Hangul Day (Hangeullal) commemorates the promulgation of Hunminjeongeum and falls on October 9 each year. Its origins trace to 1926, when the Joseon Language Research Society (Joseoneo Yeonguhoe) designated the 480th anniversary of the promulgation as "Gagya Day" (Gagyanal), observed on the 29th day of the ninth lunar month. The name was changed to "Hangul Day" in 1928. It is currently a legal national holiday in the Republic of Korea.

Basis for the Date

The Sejong Sillok (Annals of King Sejong) records the promulgation of Hunminjeongeum as taking place in the upper ten days of the ninth month of the 28th year of Sejong's reign (1446). The Joseon Language Research Society converted this to the solar calendar and set the date as October 29, but after liberation in 1945, the discovery of the Hunminjeongeum Haerye (1940) — which specified "the upper ten days (sanghan) of the ninth month" — led to the date being fixed as October 9 (the solar equivalent of the 10th day of the ninth lunar month).

Holiday History

After liberation in 1945, October 9 was designated a public holiday. However, in 1991 it was removed from the holiday calendar — along with Armed Forces Day and Constitution Day — on the grounds that there were too many public holidays. Demands from Hangul organizations and the public for its restoration followed, and from 2013 it was reinstated as a legal national holiday.

Key Facts

Origin: "Gagya Day" in 1926 · Current date: October 9 · Designated public holiday: 1945 · Removed from holidays: 1991 · Restored: 2013
