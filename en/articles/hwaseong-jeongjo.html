Hwaseong Fortress and King Jeongjo — A Fortress for His Father, a City for Reform

1794–1796. Jeongjo built Hwaseong Fortress beside his father Crown Prince Sado's tomb. More than a fortress — a dream of a new political capital.

Era Joseon Topic Architecture & Culture Topic People Region Suwon

Overview

Hwaseong (華城) is the fortress of Suwon, Gyeonggi Province, whose construction began in January 1794 and was completed in September 1796. King Jeongjo (正祖, r. 1776–1800) relocated his father Crown Prince Sado's tomb (Hyeollyungwon, now Yungrŭng) to Suwon and built a new city and fortress alongside it. With a total wall perimeter of 5.7 km, four gates, and advanced defensive installations such as cannon towers (poru) and hollow bastions (gongsimdon), it was the most technologically sophisticated fortress in East Asia at the time. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1997.

Jeong Yak-yong and the Geojunggi

Jeongjo entrusted the design of Hwaseong to Jeong Yak-yong (丁若鏞). Drawing on Chinese technical manuals, Jeong devised the geojunggi (擧重機, a pulley-based device for moving heavy loads) and the nokro (轆轤, a type of crane). Thanks to these mechanical devices, construction that would have taken ten years was completed in just two and a half. The entire construction process is meticulously documented in the Hwaseong Seongyeok Uigwe (華城城役儀軌), which served as the basis for modern restoration work.

Jeongjo's Vision and Its End

Hwaseong was not merely a fortress. Jeongjo intended to make it a new political capital — to break the Noron Byeokpa faction's grip on power in Hanyang (Seoul) and strengthen royal authority. However, Jeongjo died suddenly in 1800, and this plan was left unfinished.

Key Facts

Construction began: January 1794 · Completed: September 1796 (2 years, 8 months) · Perimeter: 5.7 km · Designer: Jeong Yak-yong · Geojunggi (pulley crane) used · UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1997
