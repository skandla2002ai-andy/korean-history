The Science of Hunminjeongeum — Why Hangul Is Easy to Learn

Consonants modeled on the shape of vocal organs. Vowels built on three principles: sky, earth, and humanity. The world's only writing system whose design principles are fully documented.

Era Joseon Topic Hangul · Linguistics

Overview

Hunminjeongeum (訓民正音), now called Hangul, was created by King Sejong (r. 1418–1450) in 1443 and promulgated in 1446. The original script comprised 17 consonants and 11 vowels (modern Hangul uses 14 consonants and 10 vowels). The explanatory manual Hunminjeongeum Haerye (解例本, 1446), which documents the design principles and usage, survives to this day. It was registered as a UNESCO Memory of the World in 1997.

Design Principles

Hangul consonants were modeled on the shape of the vocal organs. ㄱ represents the root of the tongue blocking the throat; ㄴ represents the tip of the tongue touching the gum; ㅁ represents the shape of the lips; ㅅ represents the shape of the teeth; ㅇ represents the shape of the throat. Strokes are added systematically to differentiate sounds — ㄱ becomes ㅋ, then ㄲ. Vowels are built by combining the three philosophical principles of sky (·), earth (ㅡ), and humanity (ㅣ).

International Recognition

Linguists rank Hangul among the most scientifically designed writing systems in the world. The direct correspondence between vocal organs and letter shapes, the systematic addition of strokes, and the regularity of vowel-consonant combinations are the basis of this assessment. In 1989, the Oxford Companion to the English Language selected Hangul as the world's best writing system for its rationality, originality, and efficiency. It is internationally known by the romanization "Hangul."
