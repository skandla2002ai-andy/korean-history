History of Gaeseong — 500 Years as the Goryeo Capital

Gaeseong served as the Goryeo capital for 500 years from 918. Manwoldae palace, Seonjukgyo bridge, and the millennium legacy of Gaeseong merchants.

Chosen as the Goryeo Capital

Gaeseong was the hometown of Wang Geon (founder of Goryeo) and lay within the former sphere of influence of Taebong, the state founded by Gung Ye. After ascending the throne in 918, Wang Geon designated this place — then called Songak (松岳) — as his capital, initially naming it Gaeju (開州); it was later renamed Gaekyeong (開京) during the reign of King Seongjong. Geomantically, the basin was shielded by Songaksan Mountain to the north and watered by the Imjin and Han rivers to the south, offering strong natural defenses while being well-positioned to access the Yellow Sea trade routes via the Yeseong River.

Manwoldae and the Capital's Character

Manwoldae (滿月臺), the Goryeo royal palace complex, was a large walled palace built on the southern slopes of Songaksan. Only foundation stones and terraces remain today, but intermittent joint North–South Korean excavations since 2007 have yielded thousands of artifacts. Within the capital stood dozens of Buddhist temples — including Gaeguksa and Heungwangsa — and the Gukjagam, Goryeo's highest national educational institution. Seonjukgyo (善竹橋), the bridge where loyal Goryeo minister Jeong Mong-ju was assassinated at the end of the dynasty, still stands in present-day Gaeseong.

Gaeseong Merchants and Economic Legacy

Gaeseong was also renowned as a commercial city. Known as the "Songsang" (松商), Gaeseong merchants dominated national distribution networks from the Goryeo period through the Joseon period. Their distinctive double-entry bookkeeping system, the "Sagae Songdo Chibubeopso," is recognized internationally as an independently developed accounting system. Gaeseong's signature product — Goryeo ginseng (Korean ginseng) — was exported to China, Japan, and the western regions, driving Goryeo's foreign trade. This commercial tradition continued after the Korean division through the Gaeseong Industrial Complex (2004–2016).
