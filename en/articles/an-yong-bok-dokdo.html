An Yong-bok and the Defense of Dokdo — A Fisherman Who Defended His Country

In 1693, An Yong-bok, a fisherman from Dongnae, was taken to Japan. He crossed to Japan twice to assert that Ulleungdo and Dokdo belonged to Joseon. The state punished him for it.

Era Joseon Topic Territory · People

Overview

An Yong-bok (安龍福, dates unknown) was a fisherman from Dongnae during the reign of King Sukjong of Joseon (late 17th century). He is notable for directly protesting to Japan over Joseon's sovereignty over Ulleungdo and Dokdo. In 1693, after a confrontation with Japanese fishermen, he was taken to Japan and later returned. In 1696, he voluntarily traveled to the Oki Islands to obtain an acknowledgment from the local daimyo that Ulleungdo and Dokdo were Joseon territory. Upon his return, he was punished for traveling to Japan without authorization but was spared the death penalty.

Two Visits to Japan

During his first visit in 1693, An Yong-bok claimed to have received a diplomatic document (書契, seogyae) from the Japanese shogunate stating that Ulleungdo did not belong to Japan. During his second visit in 1696, he styled himself "Inspector of Ulleungdo and Usando" and personally protested to the Oki Island lord. His activities are credited with influencing Japan's 1696 prohibition on Tottori domain residents from traveling to Ulleungdo.

Historical Significance and Limitations

An Yong-bok's actions are used as historical evidence that Joseon recognized Ulleungdo and Dokdo as its own territory. However, some of his accounts have been challenged as potentially exaggerated or fabricated. The Joseon court at the time maintained a policy of evacuating islanders to the mainland (쇄환정책, swaehwan policy).

Key Facts

First visit to Japan: 1693 (abducted) · Second visit to Japan: 1696 (voluntary) · Punishment upon return: exile · Japan's prohibition on traveling to Ulleungdo: 1696
