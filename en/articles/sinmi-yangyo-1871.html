Sinmi Yangyo — First Armed Clash with the United States

The U.S. Asiatic Squadron's assault on Ganghwa Island in 1871. The first military clash between Korea and the United States, and the hardening of Korea's isolationist policy.

Era Late Joseon Topic War Topic Diplomacy Related United States

The U.S. Fleet's Attack on Ganghwa Island

The Sinmi Yangyo (辛未洋擾) was a military incursion carried out by the U.S. Asiatic Squadron against Ganghwa Island in 1871, the eighth year of King Gojong's reign. The United States demanded reparations for the General Sherman incident of 1866 — in which an American merchant vessel was burned by the people and soldiers of Pyongyang on the Daedong River — and insisted on opening Korea to trade. The squadron entered the straits of Ganghwa with five warships and approximately 1,230 men.

Battles at Chojijin and Gwangseongbo Fort

Armed conflict broke out when Korean shore batteries opened fire on the American fleet first. U.S. forces captured Chojijin and Deokjinjin in succession, then attacked Gwangseongbo Fort. The Korean garrison commanded by General Eo Jae-yeon fought to the last man, resisting with stones and bare hands after their ammunition ran out. The Americans suffered three killed and about ten wounded, while Korean casualties numbered in the hundreds. The U.S. forces won militarily but failed to open negotiations and withdrew.

Hardened Isolationism and the Cheokwha Steles

Following the Sinmi Yangyo, the Heungseon Daewongun erected cheokwha steles (斥和碑, "repel the foreigners" monuments) throughout the country to proclaim Korea's determination to remain closed. Bearing the inscription "If Western barbarians invade and we do not fight, we must be seeking peace — and seeking peace is selling the nation," these steles became the symbol of the Daewongun's policy of rejecting any opening to the outside world. The command banner of General Eo Jae-yeon, bearing the character 帥 (commander), was taken to the United States after the battle at Gwangseongbo and was returned on long-term loan in 2007.

Historical Significance

The Sinmi Yangyo demonstrated both the rigidity of Joseon's isolationist policy and the stark military gap between Joseon and Western powers armed with modern weapons. Korea's formal opening to the outside world ultimately came not through this American expedition but by Japanese coercion in 1876.
