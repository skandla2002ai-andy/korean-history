Dangun and Gojoseon — Between Myth and History

The founding myth places Dangun in 2333 BCE. Yet the Gojoseon that historical scholarship can verify dates from the 7th–4th centuries BCE. Myth is not history — but it is where history begins.

Era Gojoseon Topic Foundation Myth · History

Overview

Gojoseon (古朝鮮) is the first state in Korean history. The founding myth — that Dangun Wanggeom (檀君王儉) established it in 2333 BCE at Asadal (阿斯達) — is recorded in the Samguk Yusa (三國遺事, c. 1281) and Jewang Ungi (帝王韻紀, 1287). Historically, the name "Joseon" appears in written Chinese records from around the 7th to 4th centuries BCE. Gojoseon was conquered by the Han dynasty of China in 108 BCE.

The Content of the Dangun Myth

Hwanung, the son of Heaven, descended to Mount Taebaek and established Sinsi (the Sacred City). A bear and a tiger both wished to become human. Hwanung gave them mugwort and garlic and told them to endure 100 days in a cave. The tiger gave up; the bear persevered and became Ungnyeo (Bear Woman). The son of Hwanung and Ungnyeo was Dangun Wanggeom. Dangun established his capital at Asadal and founded the state of Joseon.

Myth and History

Reading the Dangun myth as literal history is not academically supported. However, the historical meaning embedded in the myth is significant. Some interpret it as symbolizing the union of a bear-worshipping tribe (the Ye-Maek people) with the Hwanung group. The date 2333 BCE was recorded in the 13th century, during the Goryeo period; it is possible that Goryeo people systematized it in order to claim the antiquity of their nation's history.

Key Facts

Date in founding myth: 2333 BCE · Source: Samguk Yusa (c. 1281) · First historical record of "Joseon": 7th–4th century BCE Chinese texts · Fall: 108 BCE (conquered by Han China)
