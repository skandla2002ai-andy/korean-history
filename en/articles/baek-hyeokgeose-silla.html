Park Hyeokgeose — The Founding Myth of Silla and the King Born from an Egg

Park Hyeokgeose, who founded Silla in 57 BCE. The myth of a king born from an egg and the historical significance of the kingdom's founding.

Era Three Kingdoms Topic People Topic History Region Gyeongju

Overview

Park Hyeokgeose (朴赫居世, 69 BCE – 4 CE) is recorded in the Samguk Sagi and Samguk Yusa as the founding ruler who established Silla in 57 BCE. According to his birth legend, a large, luminous egg was discovered near the Najeong Well in Gyeongju, and from it a boy was born. The people acclaimed him king. His surname "Park (朴)" was chosen because the egg resembled a gourd (박, pak), and his given name "Hyeokgeose (赫居世)" means "to illuminate and govern the world."

The Founding Myth and Its Historical Context

The egg-birth myth of Park Hyeokgeose shares a structural pattern with similar legends about Jumong of Goguryeo and King Suro of Gaya, all emphasizing a divine bloodline descended from heavenly gods. This reflects a common pattern in ancient East Asian founding myths that legitimized the authority of new rulers as heaven-ordained.

Historically, early Silla was in a stage known as the Saro confederacy, a coalition of small states centered on the Gyeongju basin. Park Hyeokgeose was the first leader of this alliance, and represents the starting point of Silla's unique political structure in which royal power alternated among three clans: Park, Seok, and Kim. His queen, Alyeong, is also said to have been born from a well — a detail reflecting Silla's ritual tradition of venerating paired male and female sacred figures.

Historical Significance

The Park Hyeokgeose myth served as the basis for the Silla royal family's legitimacy and ancestral veneration. The Five Royal Tombs (Oreung) in Gyeongju are traditionally held to be the burial mounds of Park Hyeokgeose and other early Silla kings, and are today designated as Historic Site No. 172. The founding narrative, transmitted through the symbolic language of egg-birth mythology, is an important cultural heritage reflecting the worldview and political ideology of ancient Koreans.
