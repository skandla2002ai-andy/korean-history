Founding of Won Buddhism — Sotaesan Bak Jung-bin

Won Buddhism, founded in 1916 in Jeolla Province by Bak Jung-bin. A Korean-born religion that brings Buddhist teaching into everyday life.

Era Modern Topic Religion Topic Person Region Jeolla

Sotaesan Bak Jung-bin and the Great Enlightenment

Won Buddhism (圓佛敎) is a Korean-born religion founded by Sotaesan (少太山) Bak Jung-bin (朴重彬, 1891–1943). Born in Yeonggwang, South Jeolla Province, Bak had questioned the fundamental nature of the universe and human life from childhood, spending his youth in spiritual seeking. On April 28, 1916, after years of arduous practice, he declared that he had attained Great Enlightenment (大覺) — the realization that "all existence shares one essence and all dharmas share one source." Won Buddhism commemorates this date as the Festival of Great Enlightenment and the Opening of the Teaching. Sotaesan confirmed that his awakening was consistent with the teachings of Shakyamuni Buddha, and on that foundation he established a new religious system that took Buddhism as its primary intellectual core while integrating Confucianism, Taoism, and Donghak.

Core Teachings and Characteristics

The symbol of Won Buddhism, the Il-Won-Sang (一圓相, ○ — "One Circle"), represents the fundamental truth of all existence in the universe. Unlike traditional Buddhism, Won Buddhism emphasizes "everyday Buddhism" — cultivating the Way in the midst of ordinary life, without distinctions between monastics and laypeople. The motto "As material civilization has opened up, let us also open up spiritual civilization" reveals the movement's character as a call to spiritual awakening in response to the material advances of modernity. Sotaesan also pursued practical life-reform campaigns: abolishing empty formalities, combating superstition, prohibiting alcohol and tobacco, and promoting saving. He further championed gender equality and the spread of education, becoming socially progressive from an early stage — for example, by training female religious ministers (gyomu) from the beginning.

Growth and Development of the Order

In 1924 Sotaesan established the central headquarters of the order in Iksan, North Jeolla Province, formally organizing the institution. Despite repression under Japanese colonial rule, the order grew steadily, expanding into education, hospitals, and social welfare work. After Sotaesan's death, Jungsan (鼎山) Song Gyu (宋奎) led the order as its second head patriarch, and in 1947 the official name "Won Buddhism" was confirmed. Since then, Won Buddhism has spread not only throughout Korea but also to the United States, Japan, Europe, and other parts of the world; its domestic membership is estimated at approximately one million today.

Historical Significance

Along with Donghak and Jeungsangyo, Won Buddhism is one of the most systematically institutionalized of the Korean-born new religions that emerged in the period following the opening of Korea to the outside world. Won Buddhism's model — combining Buddhist reform, everyday ethics, and social engagement — stands as an important example of the diversity of Korean religious culture in the twentieth century.
