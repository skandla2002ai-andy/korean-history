The Baekje Restoration Movement — The Resistance of Heukchi Sangji and Boksin

The three-year restoration movement that followed Baekje's fall in 660 CE. Through to the Battle of Baekgang, supported by Wa Japan.

Era Three Kingdoms Topic War Topic Politics Related Japan Related China

Resistance Immediately After the Fall — Boksin and Dochim

When Sabi fortress fell in August 660 and King Uija was taken to Tang China, Baekje survivors immediately began their resistance. The central figures were Boksin (福信), a general of aristocratic birth, and the monk Dochim (道琛). Using Imjonseong (Yesan, South Chungcheong) and Juryuseong (believed to be Seocheon or Buan, South Chungcheong) as their bases, the two men stood against the Silla–Tang occupation forces. Boksin summoned Prince Buyeo Pung (扶餘豊), son of King Uija who had been residing in Wa (Japan), to return and be enthroned as king, providing a legitimizing cause for rebuilding the state. At its peak, the movement commanded the allegiance of over 200 fortresses, and the Silla–Tang forces were unable to easily subdue Juryuseong.

Heukchi Sangji (黑齒常之) was another key figure in the restoration movement. A military commander of the rank of Dalsolsol, he surrendered in 660 but quickly returned to resistance upon witnessing Tang plunder, assembling 30,000 soldiers at Imjonseong and repelling Tang attacks multiple times. Heukchi Sangji's exploits represented some of the most brilliant military achievements in the early phase of the restoration movement.

The Battle of Baekgang and the End of the Restoration Movement

In August 663, Wa dispatched a naval force of 400 ships and 27,000 men to support Buyeo Pung. The Silla–Tang forces met the Wa navy at Baekgang (白江, the mouth of the Geum River). The Silla–Tang fleet won a decisive victory, burning all 400 Wa ships — a battle known as the Battle of Baekgang (or in Japanese history, the Battle of Hakusukinoe). This naval engagement was a large-scale clash of international dimensions, significant in both Korean and Japanese history. With the Wa forces destroyed, Buyeo Pung fled to Goguryeo, and as Juryuseong fell the restoration movement effectively ended. Boksin was executed by Buyeo Pung in an internal power struggle; Dochim had already been killed by Boksin. Heukchi Sangji eventually surrendered to Tang and served as a Tang general.

Historical Significance

The Baekje restoration movement was an intense three-year resistance that followed immediately upon the kingdom's fall. Some historians suggest the outcome might have been different had the leadership not fractured. The movement demonstrated the Baekje survivors' national identity and will to rebuild their state during the turbulent period when Silla and Tang competed for dominance over the Korean Peninsula and East Asia. The defeat at Baekgang prompted Wa to abandon ambitions on the peninsula and turn instead toward consolidating its domestic order — a shift that had profound effects on the East Asian international order.
