The April Revolution — Students Bring Down a Dictatorship

April 19, 1960. Students stood before gunfire and did not retreat. Twelve years of Rhee Syngman's dictatorship came to an end.

Era Modern Topic Democracy · Revolution

Overview

The April Revolution (四一九革命, 1960) was a democratic uprising by students and citizens protesting the fraudulent March 15 elections staged by Rhee Syngman's Liberal Party. Police opened fire on April 19, killing 185 people and wounding thousands. On April 26, President Rhee issued a statement of resignation, bringing the dictatorship to an end.

The Trigger — The Rigged Election of March 15

In the presidential and vice-presidential elections of March 15, 1960, the Liberal Party carried out blatant fraud: ballot-box stuffing, open voting in groups of four, and the deployment of intimidation squads. Protests erupted in Masan. On April 11, the body of missing high school student Kim Ju-yeol was found floating in the sea off Masan — a tear-gas canister lodged in his eye — and the nation exploded in outrage.

April 19 and the Resignation

On April 19, tens of thousands of students and citizens marched toward Gyeongmudae (the presidential residence) in Seoul. Police opened fire, killing 185 people. On April 25, university professors issued a declaration of conscience and took to the streets. The following day, Rhee issued his resignation and went into exile in Hawaii.

Key Facts

Trigger: rigged election of March 15, 1960 · Kim Ju-yeol's body found: April 11, 1960 · Deaths from gunfire: 185 (April 19) · Rhee's resignation: April 26, 1960 · Military coup ending the new democratic government: May 16, 1961
